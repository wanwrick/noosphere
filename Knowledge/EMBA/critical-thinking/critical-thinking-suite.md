# Critical Thinking Suite — IDEALS · Know/Assume/Don't Know · 10-10-10 · System 1/2

> Foundational frameworks for problem-framing and decision-quality. Use before solving; the most expensive mistake is solving the wrong problem.

## IDEALS — Problem identification (Browne & Keeley)

| Letter | Question |
|---|---|
| **I** Identify | What is the problem? State it in plain English. |
| **D** Define | Define key terms. Make implicit assumptions explicit. |
| **E** Enumerate | What are the choices / options? |
| **A** Analyze | Pros, cons, evidence for each option. |
| **L** List reasons | Why is the chosen option preferred? |
| **S** Self-correct | What would change my mind? What's the strongest counter-argument? |

The S step is the load-bearing one. Most decision processes skip it.

## Know / Assume / Don't Know

A three-column inventory before any consequential decision:

| Know | Assume | Don't Know |
|---|---|---|
| Things with evidence | Things you believe but haven't verified | Open questions you'd want to answer |

The middle column is where bad decisions live. Move "Assume" → "Know" by gathering evidence; move "Don't Know" → "Assume" or "Know" by deciding which gaps matter.

A decision made entirely from "Know" is rare and usually too late. A decision made mostly from "Assume" is fragile. The discipline is to know **which** assumptions you're betting on.

## 10-10-10 (Suzy Welch)

Three time-frames for evaluating a decision:

- **10 minutes** — How will you feel about this in 10 minutes? (immediate emotional response)
- **10 months** — How will you feel about this in 10 months? (medium-term consequence)
- **10 years** — How will you feel about this in 10 years? (long-term identity / value alignment)

Tactical for pressured decisions: most things that feel urgent at 10 minutes don't matter at 10 months.

## System 1 / System 2 (Kahneman)

| System | Mode | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| **System 1** | Fast, automatic, intuitive | Pattern-matching · low effort · prone to bias |
| **System 2** | Slow, deliberate, analytical | Reasoning · high effort · effortful |

For high-stakes decisions: deliberately engage System 2 by writing down reasoning, listing assumptions, exposing the chain of logic. System 1 is fine for low-stakes; System 2 is mandatory for irreversible.

## Common cognitive biases to watch for

- **Confirmation bias** — seek disconfirming evidence first.
- **Availability heuristic** — recent vivid examples skew probability estimates.
- **Anchoring** — first number heard biases all subsequent estimates.
- **Sunk cost fallacy** — ignore costs already incurred; decide on future expected value.
- **Status quo bias** — change has costs; *not* changing also has costs. Weigh both.

## When to use

- Any consequential decision (irreversible, costly, multi-stakeholder).
- Strategic prioritization (which initiative to fund).
- Diagnostic framing (what is the actual problem?).
- Pre-mortem reviews (what would make this fail?).
