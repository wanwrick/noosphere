# Negotiation Map — Shai Dubey (Cornell EMBA)

> Map every party's stated position **and** underlying interest. Their stated position is rarely their actual interest.

## The map

Three columns per stakeholder:

| Stakeholder | Stated position | Underlying interest |
|---|---|---|
| You | "I want X" | "I need [outcome that X would deliver]" |
| Adjacent party | "We already own Y" | "We need to maintain [relevance / scope / headcount]" |
| Decision-maker | "I want clean ownership" | "I need [no manager wars / clear escalation / capability]" |

The discipline is to fill in column 3 honestly — including for yourself.

## Build the BATNA

**BATNA** (Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement) — what happens if no deal? In platform / cross-team negotiations, the BATNA is often "we do the work without the title." That's a strong BATNA because the technical win exists either way; the question is whether the formal mandate follows.

A strong BATNA is leverage. A weak BATNA is a tell.

## Coalition-building sequence

```
Internal alignment (engineers / direct team)
        ↓
Adjacent-team brief (heads-up, NOT negotiation)
        ↓
Manager calibration
        ↓
Coordinated proposal to decision-maker
```

The "heads-up, not negotiation" step is non-obvious. Adjacent teams need to know what you're going to ask before the decision-maker hears it, but the briefing is informational, not permission-seeking. You are not asking permission; you are confirming you have not surprise-stepped on their scope.

## Briefing pattern (for the heads-up)

> "I'm going to [decision-maker] to formalize [Layer X] ownership. [Adjacent team] continues to own [their thing]. I'm taking [the specific scope I'm asking for]. I want to position this as cleaning up scope together, not a turf question."

The phrasing matters. *Clean up scope together* is collaborative. *Take* is acquisitive.

## Common traps

- **Negotiating from your stated position.** Always negotiate from the underlying interest.
- **Skipping the adjacent-team brief.** They will hear about your ask third-hand and read it as a turf grab.
- **Treating BATNA as a threat.** It's leverage, not a weapon. Mention it only if asked.
- **Asking for the title before showing the work.** Reverse the order.
