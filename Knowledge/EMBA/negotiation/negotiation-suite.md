# Negotiation Suite — BATNA · ZOPA · Principled · Cornell Playbook

> Foundational negotiation frameworks. Pair with `negotiation-map-shai-dubey.md` for stakeholder mapping.

## BATNA — Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement

The course of action you'll take if this negotiation fails. BATNA is your **walk-away point**: any deal worse than your BATNA, you should walk; any deal better, you should consider.

**Rules:**
- Develop BATNA before negotiating. Walking in without one means you'll concede under pressure.
- A strong BATNA is leverage; a weak BATNA is exposure.
- Improve BATNA before negotiating, not during. (Generate alternative offers; cultivate adjacent options.)
- Don't reveal your BATNA unless asked, and even then frame it carefully.

## ZOPA — Zone of Possible Agreement

The overlap between your reservation price and the counterparty's reservation price. If reservation prices don't overlap, **no deal is possible** — keep negotiating means wasting time.

```
Your reservation       Counterparty's reservation
       ↓                          ↓
[──────────────[========ZOPA========]──────────────]
       Their best                  Your best
```

If the ZOPA is wide, both sides can leave value on the table. If narrow, every concession matters. If non-existent, negotiate the alternatives (terms, timing, scope) instead of price.

## Principled Negotiation (Fisher & Ury — *Getting to Yes*)

Four pillars:

1. **Separate people from problem.** Your relationship with the counterparty is a separate issue from the substantive question. Address the relationship; negotiate the substance.
2. **Focus on interests, not positions.** Position = "I want $1M." Interest = "I need to fund the next quarter without dilution." Interests open creative trades; positions create deadlock.
3. **Generate options for mutual gain.** Brainstorm before evaluating. Joint problem-solving over zero-sum bargaining.
4. **Insist on objective criteria.** Anchor outcomes to standards (market price, precedent, expert opinion) not to who pushes harder.

## Cornell EMBA Negotiation Playbook (extends Shai Dubey)

Six-step session structure:

1. **Pre-meeting prep** — fill in the negotiation map (stakeholder positions + interests) and BATNA / ZOPA.
2. **Open with relationship** — establish trust before substance.
3. **Surface interests** — ask why; listen for underlying needs.
4. **Generate options** — joint brainstorming; defer evaluation.
5. **Evaluate against objective criteria** — anchor decisions to standards.
6. **Close with explicit agreement** — write down what was agreed; circulate within 24 hours.

## Common traps

- **Negotiating from positions, not interests.** Locks both sides into win/lose framing.
- **Failing to separate people from problem.** Personal frustration colors substantive judgment.
- **No BATNA preparation.** Means you concede when pressured.
- **Anchoring too low or too high.** First-offer effect is real; bring objective basis.
- **Ignoring the cultural dimension.** Hofstede's dimensions matter (power distance, individualism, uncertainty avoidance).
