# Cornell 5-Phase Strategic Process (NBAB5630)

> The strategic-planning process taught in the Cornell-Queen's EMBA NBAB5630 course. Five phases with feedback loops. Used end-to-end for annual strategy or strategic resets.

## The five phases

```
1. Environmental Analysis
        ↓
2. Strategy Formulation
        ↓
3. Strategy Implementation
        ↓
4. Evaluation & Control
        ↓
5. Feedback to Phase 1
```

## Phase 1 — Environmental Analysis

Tools (use 2–3, not all):

- **PESTLE** — political · economic · social · technological · legal · environmental.
- **Porter's 5F** — industry attractiveness.
- **VRIN/VRIO** — internal capability assessment.
- **Value Chain** — where value is created internally.
- **CAGE** — for international questions.

**Output:** Situation analysis. Single page. What's true about the world that shapes our choices.

## Phase 2 — Strategy Formulation

Tools:

- **Strategy Diamond** (Hambrick) — five elements equilibrium check.
- **Roger Martin Cascade** — five choices in sequence.
- **Blue Ocean** — new-market creation.
- **Discovery-Driven Planning** — high-uncertainty ventures.
- **Strategic Health Matrix** — portfolio prioritization.

**Output:** A clear posture. Where to play, how to win, supporting capabilities.

## Phase 3 — Strategy Implementation

Tools:

- **Kotter's 8-Step** — change-management sequence.
- **Stakeholder Analysis** — power × interest grid.
- **Balanced Scorecard** — financial · customer · process · learning measures.
- **Resource allocation** — budget + headcount aligned to choices.

**Output:** Plan with owners, milestones, decision rights, and resource commitments.

## Phase 4 — Evaluation & Control

Tools:

- **KPIs** aligned to the Balanced Scorecard.
- **Stage-gate reviews** at predefined checkpoints.
- **Variance analysis** — actual vs plan; ask why.
- **Pre-mortem / post-mortem** — structured learning.

**Output:** Disciplined assessment of whether the strategy is working.

## Phase 5 — Feedback Loop

Failures or surprises in Phase 4 feed back into Phase 1. The environment changed; the strategy may need to evolve. **Strategy is not "set it and forget it"** — it is iterative.

## When to use

- Annual strategic-planning cycle.
- Major external shock (regulatory change, competitive entry, technology shift).
- New CEO / CDO / CTO arrival; need to set posture.
- Post-merger integration.

## When *not* to use

- Tactical operational decisions.
- Within-year re-prioritization (use a faster cycle like OODA).
