## #1 Rule: PREPARE
> "Failing to prepare is preparing to fail." — John Wooden

### Quick Prep Sheet
```
1. Purpose of negotiation
2. Your BATNA ←→ Their BATNA (estimate)
3. Your Interests & Priorities ←→ Their Interests (estimate)
4. Shared interests vs. Opposed interests
5. Your Reservation Point ←→ Their Reservation Point
6. Your Target/Goal ←→ Their Target/Goal
7. Options for mutual gain
8. Fairness standards / objective criteria
9. First 180 (opening moves)
10. 2nd Table (their constituents, constraints)
```

## Core Frameworks

### BATNA
Your walk-away option. Stronger BATNA = more power. Always assess BOTH sides.

### ZOPA
Zone of Possible Agreement = overlap between reservation points.

### Anchoring
- Go first when well-informed. Use precise numbers ($98,500 > $100,000).
- **Defense:** Ignore their anchor → focus on their BATNA → extreme counter → propose moderation.

### Three Types of Interests
| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| **Substantive** | Money, property, tangible benefits |
| **Procedural** | How the dispute is handled |
| **Psychological** | Trust, respect, fairness, status |

> "Most deals are 50% emotion and 50% economics."

### Integrative Negotiation (4 Processes)
1. Create free flow of information
2. Put aside assumptions
3. Understand their real needs
4. Search for solutions meeting both parties' goals

**Key:** Be firm on interests, flexible on method. Use "supposals" not positions.

## Dealing with Difficult Negotiators

### YES-NO-YES Framework
1. **YES:** "This is important for me/us"
2. **NO:** "I won't negotiate this way"
3. **YES:** "Here's what I propose instead"

### De-escalation Sequence
Listen → Acknowledge → "Yes...and" (never "But") → Counter with a question → Offer choices → Help them save face

### Power Disadvantage? Reframe:
> "What will they do without ME?" not "What will I do without them?"

## Ten Frames (Lewicki/Dubey)

| Frame | Watch For |
|---|---|
| Substantive | Misdiagnosing the real issue |
| Outcome | Tunnel vision on one number |
| Aspiration | ✅ Best for value creation |
| Loss-Gain | Loss frame → risky behavior |
| Identity + Characterization | "Skewed vision" — us vs. them |
| Rights | Hardest frame to shift |
| Power | Damages relationships |
| Interests | ✅ Best for integrative deals |

**Frames are dynamic. Reframing is a strategic necessity.**

## Six Habits of Merely Effective Negotiators (Sebenius)
1. Neglecting BATNAs
2. Searching too hard for common ground
3. "It's their problem!"
4. Letting positions drive out interests
5. Neglecting the second table
6. Not adjusting for context

## Biases to Watch
| Bias | Trap |
|---|---|
| Mythical Fixed-Pie | Assuming everything is zero-sum |
| Irrational Escalation | Doubling down because you committed |
| Winner's Curse | Feeling you left money on the table |
| Reactive Devaluation | Rejecting ideas just because they proposed them |
| Selective Perception | Only hearing what confirms your view |

## Effective Negotiator Traits
- Listen 63% of the time
- Ask 2.5× more questions
- Test & summarize 2× more
- Use ⅔ fewer irritants
- Label their behavior better
- Prefer in-person > video > phone > written

## Ethics Quick Check
1. Front-page test — would you be comfortable?
2. Golden Rule in reverse
3. Pre-plan responses to uncomfortable questions
4. Never break your word
5. Misrepresenting a material fact = lying
