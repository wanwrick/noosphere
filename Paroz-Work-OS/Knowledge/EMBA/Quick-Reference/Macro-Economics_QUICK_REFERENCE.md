## GDP — What It Measures vs. What It Misses

```
GDP = C + I + G + (X - M)
       │   │   │      │
       │   │   │      └─ Net Exports (Exports - Imports)
       │   │   └─ Government Spending
       │   └─ Investment
       └─ Private Consumption

MISSES:
  ✗ Quality improvements (light bulb paradox: cost UP, value DOWN)
  ✗ Home production (housework, caregiving)
  ✗ Free digital goods (Facebook, YouTube, Wikipedia)
  ✗ Sharing economy (Airbnb, Uber blur production boundaries)
  ✗ Environmental costs (resource depletion = income, not cost)
  ✗ Welfare/happiness (measures production, not well-being)
```

## Hyperinflation Warning Signs

```
1923 Weimar Republic → 1 trillion Marks = $1

Root Causes:
  → Gold standard abandoned to finance war
  → War debt financed by borrowing, not taxes
  → Reparations demands (Treaty of Versailles)
  → Government too weak to act
  → Industry favored cheap currency for exports
  → Fear of unemployment > fear of inflation

Consequences:
  → Savings destroyed (20-year insurance = 1 loaf of bread)
  → Social trust collapsed → political radicalization
  → Middle class devastated
  → Barter replaced monetary exchange

Resolution (Rentenmark):
  → New currency, 9 zeros removed
  → "All money is a matter of belief" (credere = to believe)
  → Central bank refused further government borrowing
```

## Jobs Report Cheat Sheet

```
BLS Survey: ~150,000 employers | Released 1st Friday of month

Confidence interval: ±120,000 jobs
  → Report of 50,000 could mean: -70,000 to +170,000
  → Only meaningful if change > 120,000

Three releases:
  Initial → Revision 1 (1 month later) → Final (2 months later)
  Average revision: ±11,000 jobs (can be much larger)

Rules:
  ☐ Never overweight a single month
  ☐ Use 3-6 month rolling averages
  ☐ Watch revisions (they tell the real story)
  ☐ Understand confidence intervals
  ☐ "Humility is the closest thing to accuracy"
```

## Currency: When Separate Currencies Make Sense

```
Friedman's Analogy: Exchange rates = daylight saving time
  → Easier to "reset the clock" (adjust exchange rate)
  → Than change thousands of individual prices/wages

FEWER CURRENCIES:             MORE CURRENCIES:
  + Lower transaction costs     + Absorb asymmetric shocks
  + Less uncertainty             + Independent monetary policy
  + No speculation target        + Local flexibility
  − Can't adjust locally        − Transaction costs
  − One-size-fits-all policy    − Uncertainty, speculation

Best for SEPARATE currencies:
  → Resource-rich nations far from dominant trading partner
  → Countries with distinct economic cycles
  → Economies subject to asymmetric shocks
```
