## 2. THE PIXL BUSINESS CASE

### Company Overview

PIXL is a DC/AC power inverter manufacturer. Teams take over management and make strategic and operational decisions across multiple simulated years, competing against other student teams.

### Product Portfolio

| Product | List Price | Notes |
|---------|-----------|-------|
| **MPI** (Mechanical Power Inverter) | $60 | Core product, established |
| **WPI** (Wind Power Inverter) | $90 | Higher-margin product, established |
| **SPI** (Solar Power Inverter) | Varies | Requires $20M cumulative R&D investment to unlock |

### Market Segments

| Segment | Description | Notes |
|---------|-------------|-------|
| **Commercial Sector** | Construction, hospitals, schools | Established market |
| **Home Energy Sector** | Residential energy market | New market opportunity, estimated $75-90M |

### Starting Challenges

1. **Insufficient sales support and advertising** — brand awareness gap
2. **Insufficient R&D investment** — falling behind competitors
3. **High reject rate** — 16% and climbing (quality/process problem)
4. **High interest rate** — cost of debt is burdensome
5. **Poor profitability** — need to improve margins and grow revenue

### Objective

**Maximize shareholder wealth**, measured by Internal Rate of Return (IRR). All decisions should be evaluated through the lens of whether they increase long-term IRR.

## 3. KEY DECISION LEVERS

### 3.1 Revenue Levers

| Lever | Description | Considerations |
|-------|-------------|----------------|
| **Pricing** | Set prices for MPI, WPI, SPI | Balance margin vs. volume; price elasticity; competitive positioning |
| **Advertising** | Invest in brand awareness | Drives demand; takes time to build awareness |
| **Sales Reps** | Hire sales representatives | Direct demand generation; also builds brand awareness |

### 3.2 R&D and Innovation

| Lever | Description | Considerations |
|-------|-------------|----------------|
| **R&D Investment** | Cumulative investment in product development | Measured relative to competition; $20M cumulative unlocks SPI |
| **Process Improvement** | Invest to reduce reject rate | ~$1M investment lowers reject rate by ~1%; direct cost reduction |

### 3.3 Production and Capacity

| Lever | Description | Considerations |
|-------|-------------|----------------|
| **Third Shift** | Add a 3rd production shift | Step-change capacity increase |
| **Overtime** | Increase hours (max 15%) | Incremental capacity; higher per-unit labor cost |
| **Capacity Expansion** | Build additional capacity | 1-year lag before new capacity is available; capital intensive |

### 3.4 Financing Decisions

| Lever | Description | Considerations |
|-------|-------------|----------------|
| **Bank Loans** | Borrow from banks | Interest rate depends on credit rating |
| **Stock Issuance** | Issue new shares (max 20% of outstanding) | Dilutes EPS; provides equity capital |
| **Stock Repurchase** | Buy back shares (max 5% of outstanding) | Boosts EPS; uses cash |
| **Dividends** | Pay cash dividends to shareholders | Signals confidence; uses cash |

### 3.5 Critical Constraint: Bank Overdraft

Avoid overdrafting the bank account at all costs. Overdraft triggers very high penalty interest rates and severely damages profitability.

## 4. STRATEGY FRAMEWORK: HAMBRICK & FREDRICKSON

### "Are You Sure You Have a Strategy?"

The Hambrick & Fredrickson strategy diamond provides a unified framework for ensuring a complete, coherent strategy. A real strategy addresses all five elements; if any is missing, the strategy is incomplete.

### The Five Elements

| Element | Core Question | Simulation Application |
|---------|--------------|----------------------|
| **Arenas** | Where will we be active? | Which product/market combinations? MPI/WPI/SPI x Commercial/Home |
| **Vehicles** | How will we get there? | Organic growth (R&D, capacity expansion, market development) |
| **Differentiators** | How will we win? | Price leadership, brand/advertising, innovation (R&D), product availability |
| **Staging** | What will be our speed and sequence? | Near-term (Years 1-3), middle-term, longer-term priorities |
| **Economic Logic** | How will we obtain our returns? | Low cost vs. premium pricing vs. customer loyalty vs. proprietary technology |

### Strategy Diamond Applied to PIXL

```
                    ARENAS
            (Product x Market choices:
             MPI, WPI, SPI x
             Commercial, Home Energy)
                     |
    VEHICLES --------+-------- DIFFERENTIATORS
 (Organic growth,    |      (Price, Brand,
  R&D investment,    |       Innovation,
  capacity builds)   |       Availability)
                     |
                  STAGING
           (Near/Mid/Long term
            sequencing of
            investments)
                     |
              ECONOMIC LOGIC
           (Low cost? Premium?
            Loyalty? Proprietary
            technology?)
```

### Strategy Coherence Test

A strong strategy has **internal consistency** across all five elements:
- Arenas should match the chosen economic logic (e.g., premium pricing requires differentiated products in markets willing to pay)
- Vehicles should support the chosen differentiators (e.g., R&D investment supports innovation-based differentiation)
- Staging should reflect resource constraints and competitive dynamics (e.g., build brand before entering new markets)

## 5. FINANCIAL ANALYSIS FRAMEWORK (SESSION 2)

### 5.1 Accrual Accounting in the Simulation

The simulation uses accrual accounting, meaning revenue is recognized when earned and expenses when incurred, not necessarily when cash changes hands. Understanding the difference between the Income Statement and Cash Flow Statement is critical.

### 5.2 Unit Economics Drilldown

The path from revenue to profitability, decomposed per unit:

```
Price per Unit
  - COGS per Unit
  = Gross Profit per Unit
  - Operating Expenses per Unit
  = Contribution Margin per Unit (Operating Profit per Unit)
  - Interest per Unit
  - Tax per Unit
  = Net Income per Unit
```

### 5.3 COGS per Unit Breakdown

```
COGS per Unit = Material Cost + Labor Cost + Depreciation + Reject Rate Cost
```

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Material Cost** | Raw material cost per unit produced |
| **Labor Cost** | Direct labor per unit (increases with overtime premium) |
| **Depreciation** | Allocated depreciation of production equipment per unit |
| **Reject Rate Cost** | Cost of defective units allocated across good units; higher reject rate = higher effective COGS |

### 5.4 Operating Expenses per Unit

```
Operating Expenses per Unit = (SG&A + Advertising + R&D + Process Improvement) / Total Units Sold
```

Operating expenses are period costs spread across total volume. Higher volume reduces the per-unit burden of fixed operating expenses (operating leverage).

### 5.5 Path to Growing EPS

```
Revenue (Price x Units)
  - COGS (Cost per Unit x Units)
  - Operating Expenses (SG&A + Advertising + R&D + Process Improvement)
  = Operating Income
  - Interest Expense
  - Income Tax
  = Net Income
  / Shares Outstanding
  = Earnings Per Share (EPS)
```

**Levers to grow EPS**:
1. **Increase revenue** — raise price (if demand holds) or increase volume
2. **Reduce COGS** — process improvement (lower reject rate), scale efficiencies
3. **Control operating expenses** — efficiency in SG&A; strategic advertising/R&D spending
4. **Optimize capital structure** — reduce interest expense through better credit rating or equity funding
5. **Share repurchase** — reduce denominator (shares outstanding)

### 5.6 Three Financial Statements

| Statement | Purpose in Simulation |
|-----------|----------------------|
| **Income Statement** | Tracks profitability: Revenue - Expenses = Net Income; basis for EPS |
| **Balance Sheet** | Snapshot of assets, liabilities, equity; monitors leverage and solvency |
| **Cash Flow Statement** | Tracks actual cash movements; critical for avoiding overdraft |

## 6. VALUATION AND PERFORMANCE MEASUREMENT

### 6.1 Internal Rate of Return (IRR)

IRR is the primary performance metric in the simulation. It represents the discount rate that makes the Net Present Value of all cash flows to shareholders equal to zero.

**Higher IRR = Better shareholder wealth creation**

### 6.2 Earnings Per Share (EPS)

```
EPS = Net Income / Shares Outstanding
```

EPS is a key driver of stock price and valuation. Growing EPS signals improving profitability on a per-share basis.

### 6.3 PE Multiple (Mauboussin Framework)

The Price-to-Earnings ratio reflects market expectations about future growth, profitability, and risk:

```
Stock Price = EPS x PE Multiple
```

**Factors affecting PE Multiple**:
- Expected growth rate (higher growth = higher PE)
- Consistency and predictability of earnings
- Risk profile (lower risk = higher PE)
- Capital efficiency (higher returns on capital = higher PE)

### 6.4 The Five Horsemen of Valuation Ratios

| Ratio | Formula | Best Used For |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **P/E** | Price / Earnings | Profitable, stable companies |
| **P/B** | Price / Book Value | Asset-heavy industries (banks, manufacturing) |
| **P/S** | Price / Sales | Early-stage or unprofitable companies with revenue |
| **EV/EBITDA** | Enterprise Value / EBITDA | Cross-capital-structure comparison; M&A valuation |
| **P/FCF** | Price / Free Cash Flow | Cash-generative businesses; most economically grounded |

### 6.5 Forecasting Uncertainty

From "The Value of Predictions" (1993): Forecasts are inherently uncertain. In the simulation, teams must make decisions under uncertainty about competitor actions, market demand, and economic conditions.

**Key principles**:
- Longer forecast horizons are less accurate
- Aggregate forecasts outperform individual ones
- Build flexibility to adapt when forecasts prove wrong

## 7. OPERATIONAL STRATEGY WITHIN THE SIMULATION

### 7.1 Capacity Planning Decision Tree

```
Is demand exceeding current capacity?
  |
  +-- YES --> Can overtime (max 15%) close the gap?
  |             |
  |             +-- YES --> Use overtime (but watch cost per unit increase)
  |             +-- NO  --> Add 3rd shift?
  |                          |
  |                          +-- YES --> Immediate capacity increase
  |                          +-- NO  --> Invest in capacity expansion (1-year lag)
  |
  +-- NO  --> Focus on margin improvement, R&D, process improvement
```

### 7.2 Reject Rate Economics

```
Current reject rate: 16% (and climbing without intervention)
Process improvement investment: ~$1M per 1% reduction

Cost of rejects = (Reject Rate / (1 - Reject Rate)) x Material + Labor per unit
```

Reducing the reject rate has a compound effect:
1. Lowers direct waste cost (fewer defective units)
2. Increases effective capacity (more sellable units from same production)
3. Improves quality reputation over time

### 7.3 R&D Strategy

R&D investment is **cumulative** and measured **relative to competition**:
- Falling behind in cumulative R&D weakens product competitiveness
- $20M cumulative R&D is the threshold to unlock SPI (Solar Power Inverter)
- R&D is a long-term differentiator — requires consistent investment over multiple rounds

## 8. FINANCIAL POLICY WITHIN THE SIMULATION

### 8.1 Capital Structure Tradeoffs

| Financing Source | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|-----------------|------------|---------------|
| **Bank Loans** | No dilution; interest is tax-deductible | Credit-rating dependent rate; increases financial risk |
| **Stock Issuance** (max 20%) | No interest burden; improves credit rating | Dilutes EPS and ownership |
| **Retained Earnings** | Free; no dilution or interest | Limited by profitability; reduces available cash |

### 8.2 Cash Management Priority

```
1. Avoid bank overdraft (highest priority — penalty interest is severe)
2. Fund operations (COGS, SG&A, required expenses)
3. Fund strategic investments (R&D, capacity, process improvement)
4. Manage debt service (interest payments, principal if due)
5. Consider shareholder returns (dividends, share repurchase)
```

### 8.3 Credit Rating Dynamics

Credit rating affects borrowing cost. Improving the credit rating lowers interest expense, which flows directly to net income and EPS.

**Factors improving credit rating**:
- Lower debt-to-equity ratio
- Consistent profitability
- Adequate cash reserves
- Equity issuance (strengthens balance sheet)

## 12. STRATEGIC PLAYBOOK TEMPLATES

### Conservative Strategy

**Arenas**: Focus on MPI + WPI in Commercial market
**Vehicles**: Organic improvement of existing operations
**Differentiators**: Low cost through process improvement and scale
**Staging**: Process improvement (Years 1-2) then gradual expansion
**Economic Logic**: Low-cost producer with efficient operations

### Aggressive Growth Strategy

**Arenas**: All products (MPI, WPI, SPI) in both Commercial and Home Energy
**Vehicles**: Heavy R&D investment, rapid capacity expansion
**Differentiators**: Innovation (SPI launch), broad market coverage
**Staging**: R&D push (Years 1-3), SPI launch and Home Energy entry (Years 3-5)
**Economic Logic**: Proprietary technology and first-mover advantage

### Balanced Strategy

**Arenas**: MPI + WPI initially, SPI when feasible, selective Home Energy entry
**Vehicles**: Measured R&D, staged capacity additions
**Differentiators**: Brand (advertising + sales reps) and moderate innovation
**Staging**: Fix reject rate (Year 1), build brand (Years 1-3), expand products/markets (Years 3+)
**Economic Logic**: Customer loyalty through quality and availability

## 13. COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

1. **Ignoring the reject rate** — The 16% reject rate is a bleeding wound; failing to invest in process improvement compounds costs every round

2. **Underfunding R&D** — Falling behind competitors in cumulative R&D erodes product competitiveness; consistent investment is required even if returns are not immediate

3. **Overdrafting the bank** — Penalty interest rates from overdraft can be devastating; always maintain a cash buffer

4. **Over-expanding capacity** — Capacity expansion has a 1-year lag; building too much capacity too early creates idle assets and depreciation drag

5. **Ignoring the strategy diamond** — Making ad hoc decisions without a coherent strategy across all five elements leads to internal inconsistency and wasted resources

6. **Chasing volume without margins** — Revenue growth means nothing if COGS and operating expenses consume all gains; monitor contribution margin per unit

7. **Neglecting financing strategy** — Ignoring the balance sheet leads to high interest costs and poor credit ratings, which erode profitability

8. **Issuing stock unnecessarily** — Equity dilution reduces EPS; only issue stock when the capital is needed for high-return investments
