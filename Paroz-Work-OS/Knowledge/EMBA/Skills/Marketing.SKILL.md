## Part I — Customer Insight Frameworks (Thomas)

### GO & STOP Signals Model
Core thesis: Customer behavior is driven by **GO signals** (triggers to buy) and **STOP signals** (barriers to purchase). Both operate at conscious and unconscious levels.

- **GO signals**: Product benefits, emotional resonance, social proof, brand trust, habit
- **STOP signals**: Price pain, risk perception, complexity, incoherence, negative associations
- **System 1** (fast, automatic, emotional) vs. **System 2** (slow, deliberate, rational) — most grocery/retail decisions involve both systems
- Key insight: Traditional market research relying on self-reports often fails because customers can't accurately report System 1 responses ("saying one thing, doing another")

### ACE Framework (Attention–Coherence–Emotion)
Evaluates how well marketing signals perform across three dimensions:

| Dimension | Question | Application |
|-----------|----------|-------------|
| **Attention** | Does it grab notice? | Salience in cluttered environments, visual distinctiveness, pattern interruption |
| **Coherence** | Does it fit expectations? | Consistency with brand image, customer goals, category norms |
| **Emotion** | Does it evoke feeling? | Positive/negative affective responses, desire, anxiety, delight |

**Usage**: Score any marketing action (packaging, ad, price, UX) on A-C-E to predict behavioral impact. Incoherence between dimensions creates STOP signals.

### Customer Insight Discovery Process
1. **Identify Behaviors to Compare**: Buyer vs. non-buyer, your product vs. competitor
2. **Map GO/STOP signals** for each behavior (product-related, customer-related, cognitive, emotional, conscious, unconscious)
3. **Apply ACE framework** to determine which signals matter most
4. **Write the insight**: One sentence capturing the driver of customer decision-making

## Part II — Brand & Product Strategy (Thomas)

### Perceptual Maps
Visual tool for category analysis showing how customers perceive competitive offerings.

**Requirements for a good perceptual map:**
- 2–3 dimensions reflecting customer perception of similarity/dissimilarity
- Identify clusters of similar/dissimilar products
- Identify **white space opportunities** (unoccupied positions)
- Can be created statistically (factor analysis, MDS) or via expert judgment

### Brand Association Maps
Network visualization of thoughts, feelings, symbols, and identities linked to a brand.

**Key principles:**
- Easier to create **coherent** new associations than incoherent ones (e.g., "value-for-money" is more coherent for Toyota Corolla than "luxury")
- Association strength can increase through conscious or nonconscious learning (e.g., product placement)
- **Strategic action**: Identify which associations to strengthen (GO signals) and which to weaken (STOP signals)

### Positioning Statement
Internal strategy document summarizing product strategy:

> **To** [target market], **Brand X is** [frame of reference] **that offers** [point of distinctiveness].

Three critical decisions:
1. **Frame of Reference** — Who are your closest competitors?
2. **Distinctiveness** — What makes your product unique? (Differentiation vs. distinctiveness)
3. **Target Market** — Which customer groups care most about your distinctiveness?

**Key distinction**: Product **differentiation** (objective feature differences) vs. **distinctiveness** (perceptual uniqueness in customer's mind)

## Part III — Financial Analysis (Thomas)

### Profitability Analysis

**Contribution Margin** = (Unit Selling Price − Unit Variable Cost) / Unit Selling Price

**Break-Even Sales** = Total Fixed Costs / Contribution Margin

**Sales to Achieve Profit Goal** = (Total Fixed Costs + Profit Goal) / Contribution Margin

*If contribution margins unavailable, use gross margins as approximation.*

### Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) & Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

**CLV** = Present value of all future profits generated from a customer over their relationship with the company.

**CAC** = Total cost of acquiring a new customer (marketing + sales expenses / new customers acquired).

**Decision rule**: Invest in customer acquisition when CLV > CAC. The ratio CLV:CAC indicates efficiency (3:1+ is healthy).

### Purchase Funnel
Predicts new product sales and sets marketing goals by modeling conversion at each stage:
- Awareness → Consideration → Trial → Purchase → Repeat → Loyalty
- Identify the **critical bottleneck** stage where conversion drops most
- Focus marketing investment on removing that specific bottleneck

### Metric Shift (Customer-Centric)
| Traditional (Product) | Customer-Centric |
|----------------------|------------------|
| Product Profitability | Customer Profitability |
| Current Sales | Customer Lifetime Value |
| Brand Equity | Customer Equity |
| Market Share | Customer Equity Share |

## Part IV — Marketing Decisions (Thomas)

### Pricing Strategy
- Is your price **coherent or incoherent** with customer expectations?
- How can you reduce the customer's **pain of paying**?
- Three steps to setting price for a new product: cost analysis, competitive benchmarking, value-based pricing
- **Loss aversion in pricing**: JC Penney's "fair and square" pricing disaster — removing discounts eliminated GO signals customers relied on

### Media Mix & Advertising
- **SOV-SOM Framework**: Share of Voice vs. Share of Market — overspending relative to market share drives growth
- **Online optimization**: Customer Acquisition Cost approach — which channel has lowest CAC? Which channels are scalable?
- **Traffic & Conversion**: Two-step digital optimization — increase traffic to site, then increase conversion rate once there

### Engineering Virality
Virality driven by creativity + network structure:
- **K factor** = # invitations from each customer × conversion rate
- Influencers responsible for ~50% of views in viral campaigns
- Influencers given monetary/non-monetary incentives

### Marketing Mix Modeling (MMM)
Statistical analysis on time series data:
Sales = Intercept + β₁(Price) + β₂(Promotions) + β₃(Distribution) + β₄(Advertising)
- Coefficients reflect sensitivity of sales to each marketing input
- Budget should shift from less effective to more effective mix elements (ROMI optimization)

## Part V — Outside-In Strategic Marketing (LaBarge)

### The Strategic Shift: Inside-Out → Outside-In
**Core thesis** (Dawar, 2013): Sources of sustainable competitive advantage have shifted downstream — from production/logistics to customer perception/experience/data.

| Inside-Out (Traditional) | Outside-In (Modern) |
|--------------------------|---------------------|
| "What else can we make and sell?" | "What else can we do for our customers?" |
| Cost leadership, product differentiation | Shape customer perceptions, build accumulative advantage |
| Product at the pinnacle | Customer needs at the pinnacle |
| Porter's Five Forces, SWOT | Customer journeys, JTBD, network effects |

### Marketing Management Philosophies (Evolution)
1. **Production Concept** → "Make"
2. **Product Concept** → "Make Better"
3. **Selling Concept** → "Make AND Sell"
4. **Market Orientation** → "Sense and Respond" (Moore, 2014)

### Innovation & Market Change Types
- **Evolutionary** — Push boundaries of existing purchase criteria
- **Generational** — Introduce new criteria complementing old ones, opening new segments
- **Revolutionary** — Introduce criteria that render old ones obsolete
*(Increasing market power required as you move from evolutionary → revolutionary)*

### Pivotal Questions Framework (Schoemaker & Krupp, 2015)
1. Think Outside In
2. Explore Future Scenarios
3. Be a Contrarian
4. Look for Patterns
5. Create New Options
6. Learn from Failure

*"Start with questions, not answers" — "If you think like everyone else, you're bound to be average."*

### Critical Fault Lines to Monitor (Bertolini, Duncan & Waldeck, 2015)
1. Customer Needs
2. Performance Metrics
3. Industry Position
4. Business Model
5. Talent and Capabilities

## Part VI — Jobs-to-be-Done (LaBarge / Christensen)

### Core Principles
- **"The customer rarely buys what the business thinks it sells him."** — Peter Drucker
- The **job**, not the customer, is the fundamental unit of analysis
- Same product may perform different jobs for different people (or multiple jobs for one person)
- **Circumstances** matter more than customer characteristics, product attributes, or trends
- Jobs are functional, social, AND emotional

### Where Traditional Segmentation Goes Wrong
1. Often product/selling oriented — upstream, not downstream
2. Too little emphasis on actual behavior and needs
3. Overemphasize the easily observable (demographics, psychographics)
4. Letting descriptions define segments (stereotyping via "personas")
5. Undue absorption in technical segmentation methods

### How to Find "Jobs"
1. **Identify problems** groups of people face — look for non-consumption, workarounds, tasks people avoid, surprising uses
2. **Combine existing solutions** — find desires consumers want to simultaneously satisfy with a single product
3. **Split existing solutions** — look for segments within categories based on goals certain consumers particularly value
4. **Beware of Feature Creep** — adding features may be irrelevant to customer needs

### Job-Market Analogy
- **Job Candidates** (products) = profile of features, benefits, brand associations
- **Employers** (customers) = specific functional/emotional/social needs to fill
- Products "apply" for the customer's job opening; positioning = tailoring the "resume"

### Elements of Value (Almquist et al., 2016/2018)
Hierarchical model of B2C and B2B value elements — functional → emotional → life-changing → social impact

## Part VII — Customer Experience & Decision Journey (LaBarge)

### From Funnel to Customer Decision Journey (CDJ)
Traditional marketing funnel assumes linear narrowing. McKinsey CDJ (Court et al., 2009) recognizes the modern journey is circular and iterative:

**Journey Stages:**
1. Need Recognition → Knows About the Brand
2. Actively Considers → Searches & Learns → Evaluates & Likes
3. Develops Willingness to Pay → Develops Plans to Buy
4. Consumes → Is Satisfied
5. Loyal Repeat Buyer → Engages → Actively Advocates

**Key shift**: Expansion of media types changed how and when consumers research, evaluate, and buy.

### Communication Objectives × CDJ × Media Type

| Objective | CDJ Stage | Media Type |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| Create awareness & salience | Need recognition, Know brand | Paid (brand ads, content), Earned (PR, influencers) |
| Convey detailed information | Consider, Search, Evaluate | Owned (website, demos, newsletters), Earned (reviews) |
| Create imagery & personality | Evaluate, WTP | Paid (video, full color), Owned (engaging content) |
| Build trust | WTP | Earned (reviews), Owned (consistent messaging, awards) |
| Elicit emotions | Know brand, Engage | Paid (broadcast), Owned (testimonials), Earned (stories) |
| Inspire action | Need recognition, Plans to buy | Paid (time-limited offers), Owned (discounts), Earned (expert reviews) |
| Instill loyalty | Satisfied, Repeat buyer | Owned (post-purchase support), Earned (ratings, awards) |
| Connect people | Engage, Advocate | Owned (community), Earned (spontaneous WOM) |

### Touchpoints & JTBD Integration
- **Consumer-initiated touchpoints**: What job is the customer trying to do? (e.g., website visit for comparison vs. post-purchase problem-solving)
- **Company-initiated touchpoints**: What job are you satisfying? (intrusive vs. addressing key needs within journey)
- **Organizing by touchpoints = inside-out thinking** — the customer doesn't care about your internal silos

## Part VIII — Design Thinking & Experimentation (LaBarge)

### Design Thinking (Brown, 2008; Stanford d.school)
Managing the "Fuzzy Front End" of innovation. Five phases:

1. **Empathize** — Learn about the audience through observation and interview
2. **Define** — Create a point of view based on user needs and insights
3. **Ideate** — Brainstorm as many creative solutions as possible
4. **Prototype** — Build a representation to show others
5. **Test** — Share prototyped idea with original users for feedback

**How Design Thinking differs**: Human-centered, iterative, bias toward action, embraces ambiguity, radical collaboration.

### Why New Products Fail
**"Fuzzy Front End" Issues:**
1. Insignificant point of differentiation
2. Ignore how customers search, interpret, decide
3. Requires customer learning but doesn't invest in it
4. Revolutionary product but no market exists yet
5. Incomplete definition of market and product

**Strategy/Execution Issues:**
1. Product falls short of claims on critical factors
2. Poor execution of marketing mix
3. Bad timing
4. Lack of economical access to buyers
5. Company can't support fast growth

### Experimentation (Thomke, 2020)
- Forward-looking, data-informed approach to strategy formation
- Goal: establish **causation**, not just correlation
- More scalable than Design Thinking (equally flexible)
- Types: Lab experiments, field experiments, A/B testing, natural experiments, market tests

**Best practices for business experiments:**
- Hypothesize change in key outcomes based on theory or data
- Use proper controls and randomization
- Measure behavioral outcomes, not self-reported preferences
- Build a **culture of experimentation** — democratize testing, invest in infrastructure

### ICE Model of Learning (Fostaty Young & Wilson, 2000)
- **Ideas**: Fundamentals, facts, discrete concepts, vocabulary
- **Connections**: Articulate new relationships, relate new learning to existing knowledge
- **Extensions**: Extrapolate to novel situations, hypothesize, understand implications

## Part IX — AI & MarTech in Marketing (LaBarge / Khoushinsky)

### AI Marketing Strategy Design (Davenport, Guha & Grewal, 2021)
Key questions for any AI marketing initiative:
1. **Data Inputs** — What data is required? (behavioral, metadata, product data, historical)
2. **Model Selection** — Classification? Clustering? Generative? Regression?
3. **Real-Time or Batch?** — Triggered vs. scheduled campaigns
4. **Evaluation Metrics** — Managerial (CTR, conversion, churn) + Technological (accuracy, latency, uptime)
5. **Deployment Method** — Integration with existing systems (ESP, CDP, dashboards)

### Common Pitfalls (Ascarza, Ross & Hardie; Mela & Cooper, 2021)
- "Why You Aren't Getting More from Your Marketing AI" — misalignment between AI capabilities and marketing objectives
- "Don't Buy the Wrong Marketing Tech" — technology acquisition without clear strategic use case

### Implementation Framework
| Use Case | Data Inputs | Model Type | Deployment |
|----------|-------------|------------|------------|
| Personalized email | Behavioral history, user metadata, campaign history | Classification + text generation | Batch (campaigns) or real-time (triggers) |
| Trend detection | Scraped images, tags, engagement metrics | CNN/ViT + clustering | Batch (weekly/monthly) |
| Customer segmentation | Transaction data, demographics, behavioral | Clustering (K-means, DBSCAN) | Batch into CRM |
| Price optimization | Sales data, competitor pricing, demand curves | Regression, reinforcement learning | Real-time or daily |

## Paroz's Applied Context

### Marketing Management Application — Dove Brand Strategy (Individual Assignment)
- Recommended 5-year strategy: strengthen emotional resonance, promote diversity, establish sustainability leadership
- **Strengthen associations** (GO signals): Empowerment, diversity, sustainability, real beauty
- **Weaken associations** (STOP signals): Basic functional benefits (moisturizing), conventional beauty standards
- Applied brand association mapping and GO/STOP framework to Dove's 2024–2029 strategy

### Marketing Strategy Application — RBAC Transformation as Design Thinking + Experimentation (Course Reflection)
Paroz's most substantive marketing strategy application — framing Questrade's RBAC transformation through LaBarge's frameworks:

**Design Thinking applied:**
- **Empathy phase**: Discovered three pain narratives — security professionals (60% admin time), business analysts (3-day data request budgeting), middle managers (hoarding access as power)
- **Reframing**: From "How do we manage 600+ groups efficiently?" → "What job are these groups doing?" (JTBD lens)
- **Core insight**: Only 27% of AD groups served legitimate security functions; 73% provided psychological comfort ("security theater")
- **Ideation**: Four-role model (Admin, Contributor, Member, Viewer) designed by those most threatened by simplification

**Experimentation applied:**
- **Finance Department pilot**: Hypothesized 60% admin reduction → achieved 65%, plus unexpected cross-functional reporting benefits
- **"Failure as data"**: Trading Operations latency issue led to caching solution through rapid iteration
- **Counterintuitive finding**: Senior executives showed highest adoption rates, not junior analysts
- **Business outcome metrics**: Decision velocity 3.5× improvement, innovation cycle 45→12 days, 85% voluntary adoption (vs. 50-60% industry)

**Integration insight — "Cognitive Consonance"**: Design Thinking built empathy; Experimentation provided proof. Together, they addressed both emotion and logic. "Diagonal influence" from CFO advocacy + peer evangelism proved more powerful than top-down mandates.

### Marketing Strategy Application — Insurance Document Automation (Post-Class Reflection 3)
- **Empathy**: Brokers spend 45-60 min manually generating documents while clients wait
- **Reframing**: From "automate documents" → "create real-time transparency that builds confidence"
- **Experiment**: 30-day controlled test — document creation dropped from 1 hour to 5 minutes, accuracy 99.2%, CSAT +23 points
- **Key insight**: Speed wasn't the primary value — reviewing documents together during the call transformed broker-client dynamic
- **Limitation**: 30% of brokers resisted despite better outcomes → successful experimentation requires human-centered design alongside analytical rigor

### Marketing Strategy Application — Peer Critique: Fieldcraft Coffee (Peer Project Critique)
Applied JTBD, positioning theory, and strategic marketing frameworks to evaluate peer team's repositioning of veteran-focused coffee brand:
- Identified fatal flaw: 45 orders/month ($13,500 annual) attempting $500M→$2B market pivot without operational foundation
- Applied **Ries & Trout positioning theory**: "Authenticity paradox" — abandoning military heritage without earning outdoor credentials creates unstable middle position
- Used **perceptual mapping** (Heritage Authenticity × Outdoor Credibility) to expose strategic vulnerability
- Recommended **2×2 matrix** (outdoor activity intensity × coffee connoisseurship) for proper segmentation
