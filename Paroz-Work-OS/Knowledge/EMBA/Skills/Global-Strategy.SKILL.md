## Module 1: What is Global Strategy & Why Firms Globalize

### CAGE Distance Framework

Four dimensions of distance between countries that affect cross-border business:

| Dimension | Examples of Distance Attributes |
|-----------|---------------------------------|
| **Cultural** | Language, religion, social norms, values (Hofstede dimensions) |
| **Administrative** | Colonial ties, political hostility, government policies, institutional weakness |
| **Geographic** | Physical distance, time zones, climate, lack of land borders |
| **Economic** | Income levels, cost/quality of resources, infrastructure, factor costs |

**Application:** Map CAGE distances onto generic strategies to determine whether to adapt, aggregate, or arbitrage.

### Three Generic Strategies for Global Value Creation (AAA)

| Strategy | Focus | Mechanism |
|----------|-------|-----------|
| **Adaptation** | Adjust to local differences | Customize products/processes for local markets |
| **Aggregation** | Exploit similarities | Achieve economies of scale across grouped markets |
| **Arbitrage** | Exploit differences | Buy low/sell high across borders (labor, capital, knowledge) |

### ADDING Value Scorecard

Six factors for assessing whether a global strategic move creates value:

1. **A**dding volume (growth)
2. **D**ecreasing costs
3. **D**ifferentiating (increasing willingness to pay)
4. **I**mproving industry attractiveness (bargaining power)
5. **N**ormalizing (optimizing) risk
6. **G**enerating knowledge (and other resources/capabilities)

### Key Case: CEMEX

Demonstrates how a cement company — an apparently localized industry — derived value from globalization through operational expertise transfer, acquisition integration capabilities, and arbitrage across emerging/developed markets.

## Module 2: Institutions & Culture

### Hofstede Cultural Distance Framework

Six dimensions:
1. Power Distance Index
2. Individualism vs. Collectivism
3. Uncertainty Avoidance Index
4. Masculinity vs. Femininity
5. Long-term vs. Short-term Orientation
6. Indulgence vs. Restraint

#### Hofstede Scores for Key EMBA Country Pairs

| Dimension | Canada | USA | India | China | Brazil | Germany | Japan |
|-----------|--------|-----|-------|-------|--------|---------|-------|
| Power Distance | 39 | 40 | 77 | 80 | 69 | 35 | 54 |
| Individualism | 80 | 91 | 48 | 20 | 38 | 67 | 46 |
| Masculinity | 52 | 62 | 56 | 66 | 49 | 66 | 95 |
| Uncertainty Avoidance | 48 | 46 | 40 | 30 | 76 | 65 | 92 |
| Long-term Orientation | 36 | 26 | 51 | 87 | 44 | 83 | 88 |
| Indulgence | 68 | 68 | 26 | 24 | 59 | 40 | 42 |

**Quick distance read:** Canada-India shows high distance on Power Distance (+38), Individualism (+32), and Indulgence (+42). Canada-US is the lowest distance pair overall. Japan scores highest on Masculinity (95) and Uncertainty Avoidance (92).

### GLOBE Framework (Additional Dimensions)

1. Performance Orientation
2. Gender Egalitarianism
3. Humane Orientation
4. Institutional Collectivism
5. Assertiveness

### Institutional Theory

**North Framework:**
1. Formal institutions (laws, regulations, constitutions)
2. Informal institutions (customs, traditions, codes of conduct)

**Scott Framework:**
1. Regulative institutions (rules, sanctions, monitoring)
2. Normative institutions (values, norms, roles)
3. Cognitive institutions (shared conceptions, cultural frames)

### SPLIT Framework (Managing Global Teams)

Types of separation between HQ and subsidiaries:
1. **S** — (implied structural separation)
2. **P**hysical separation
3. **L** — (implied language/communication)
4. **I** — (implied identity/cultural)
5. **T**emporal separation

Plus: Cultural separation, Organizational separation.

### Corruption in Foreign Markets

Types: Grand, Petty, Private, Public corruption. Each type has different impacts on foreign entry strategy and subsidiary performance. MNEs can moderate performance effects through distinct entry strategies.

### UN Global Compact (10 Principles)

- **Human Rights** (Principles 1-2): Support/respect human rights; avoid complicity
- **Labour** (Principles 3-6): Freedom of association, eliminate forced/child labor, eliminate discrimination
- **Environment** (Principles 7-9): Precautionary approach, environmental responsibility, green technology
- **Anti-Corruption** (Principle 10): Work against corruption including extortion and bribery

### Key Cases: United Cereal (Eurobrand challenge), IKEA (Indian rugs/child labor)

## Module 3: Internationalization — Location, Timing & Entry Mode

### Location Strategy: Country-Level Frameworks

**Country Portfolio Analysis (CPA):** Population/growth rate, GDP/growth rate mapped against CAGE Distance and AAA strategies.

**Firm-Level Location Framework** (Alcacer):
- **Location Characteristics:** Natural endowments, institutional environments, cluster presence, country vs. site characteristics
- **Firm Fit:** Distance, fit with capabilities, complementarity with existing locations, fit with business model
- **Government Incentives/Disincentives:** Types, willingness
- **Competitive Effect:** Competition for inputs, product competition

### Timing Strategy: Firm-Level Framework (Alcacer)

Four clocks that determine optimal entry timing:
1. **Location Clock:** Market maturity, changing conditions, foreign presence
2. **Firm Clock:** Slack resources, experience, intrinsic pace to deploy business model
3. **Incentives Clock:** Decreasing generosity, short-term vs. long-term incentives
4. **Competitive Clock:** Benefits of entering early vs. opportunity costs of postponing

### Foreign Market Entry Mode Spectrum

```
Low Control/Resource Commitment → High Control/Resource Commitment

Exporting → Licensing → Franchising → Strategic Alliance → Joint Venture → Wholly Owned Subsidiary
           (Transaction-oriented)                        (Investment-oriented)
```

### Entry Mode Performance Implications

- **JV Survival:** When MNC owns ≤20% of JV, mortality risk is 2-4x greater than WOS. At ≥40% ownership, mortality risk approaches WOS levels.
- **JV Financial Performance:** Moderate/co-ownership (50-50) JVs exhibit superior financial performance vs. both WOS and highly skewed JVs.

### Managerial Location Shunning

Managers may avoid strategically advantageous foreign locations due to personal risk aversion, unfamiliarity, or cognitive biases about "dangerous" or "difficult" markets.

### Key Cases: Globalization of the NFL, ECCO A/S (global value chain)

## Module 4: Executing a Sustainable Global Strategy

### Stakeholder Theory

- **Shareholder primacy theory** (legal theory): Firm exists to maximize shareholder value
- **Business Roundtable (2019):** Redefined corporate purpose to include all stakeholders
- **Stakeholder theory:** Firm should balance interests of all stakeholders (employees, customers, communities, shareholders, suppliers)

### Four Pillars of Sustainability-Driven Strategy (Nooyi & Govindarajan)

1. **Financial sustainability**
2. **Human sustainability** (customers)
3. **Environmental sustainability**
4. **Talent sustainability** (employees)

### Models of MNE Social Responsibility

Four postures (Bartlett & Beamish):

| Posture | Approach |
|---------|----------|
| **Exploitative** | Minimize costs, ignore social responsibility |
| **Transactional** | Comply when required, treat CSR as cost of doing business |
| **Responsive** | Proactively address stakeholder concerns, beyond compliance |
| **Transformative** | Use business model to create positive social/environmental change |

### Supply Chain Sustainability

- **Approved vendor lists:** Strategic, financial, operational, and relational advantages
- **Vendor selection criteria:** Innovation potential, environmental and social impact
- **Technology for transparency:** Uni-directional → Bi-directional → Multi-directional information flow
- Compliance vs. capability-building approaches to supplier management

### Key Cases: Unilever (USLP), Lifebuoy in India (handwashing behavior change)

## Module 5: Global R&D & Innovation Networks

### Global R&D Network Framework (Kuemmerle)

Five steps to building effective R&D capabilities abroad:
1. Link corporate strategy with innovation/R&D strategy
2. Site selection — pick optimal sites
3. Human resources — staff the sites
4. Supervise sites during start-up phase
5. Integrate activities across sites into a coordinated whole

### Discontinuous Innovation (Birkinshaw, Bessant & Delbridge)

- Implementation of dramatically new technologies, products, or business models
- Challenges: Uncertainty, routines/path dependence, inertia, allure of incremental innovation
- Requires different organizational approaches than incremental innovation

### Absorptive Capacity (Lichtenthaler & Lichtenthaler)

- **Absorptive capacity:** Ability to acquire and utilize external knowledge
- Dependent on prior knowledge base
- Three process stages

### Desorptive Capacity

- **Desorptive capacity:** Ability to identify and transfer internal knowledge externally
- Two process stages
- Enables outward technology/knowledge transfer

### Collaborative Advantage (Hansen & Nohria)

- Barriers to interunit collaboration in MNCs
- Management levers to promote collaboration
- Potential downsides of excessive collaboration

### Key Cases: Applied Research Technologies (front-line innovation), McKinsey & Co. (knowledge management)

## Module 6: Strategy-Structure Alignment

### Fundamental Strategic Tension

**Local/National Responsiveness ←→ Global Integration**

### Integration-Responsiveness (IR) Framework

```
            Global Integration
            Low                    High
Local   Low  International         Global
Resp.  High  Multidomestic        Transnational
```

### Four Strategy-Structure Types (Bartlett & Beamish)

| Strategy | Structure | HQ-Sub Relationship | Knowledge Flow |
|----------|-----------|--------------------|----|
| **Multidomestic** | Decentralized Federation | Autonomous subsidiaries | Local for local |
| **International** | Coordinated Federation | HQ transfers core competencies | Center to local |
| **Global** | Centralized Hub | Tight central control | Center outward |
| **Transnational** | Integrated Network | Differentiated contributions, shared decisions | Multidirectional |

### HQ Challenges (Baumgarten & Heywood)

1. General dissatisfaction with HQ performance
2. Excessive replication of corporate activities
3. Added cost and reduced flexibility
4. Inability to coordinate and facilitate communication

### Managing Expatriate Employees (Black & Gregersen)

Three best practices:
1. **Motivation:** Send people for the right reasons
2. **Skills:** Send the right people
3. **Repatriation:** Finish the right way

### Key Cases: Kent Chemical (organizational restructuring), Levendary Café (China market entry/HQ-subsidiary tension)

## Module 7: International Joint Ventures

### IJV Negotiation Framework (Beamish)

Six decision rules:
1. What is best for the JV (not just for either partner)?
2. What is the standard/common international price/practice?
3. Are you adopting a long-term approach or hedging?
4. What are the actual issues "to the customers"?
5. What are your indifference points?
6. Are you leaving sufficient opportunity for your partner to profit?

### Three Types of IJV Control

| Control Type | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| **Dominant Control** | One parent controls most/all major functions |
| **Shared Control** | Both parents jointly control major functions |
| **Split Control** | Each parent controls different major functions |

### Key Cases: Nora-Sakari (Malaysian telecom JV negotiation), Eli Lilly in India (JV strategy rethinking)
