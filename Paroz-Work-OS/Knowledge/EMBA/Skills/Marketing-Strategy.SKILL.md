## Module 1: Customer-Centric Marketing Strategy

### Marketing Management Philosophies (Evolution)

```
Production Concept → Product Concept → Selling Concept → Market Orientation
"Make"              "Make Better"      "Make AND Sell"    "Sense and Respond"

Product at the Pinnacle ──────────────→ Customer at the Pinnacle
```

### Inside-Out vs. Outside-In Activities

| Inside-Out (Traditional) | Outside-In (Modern) |
|--------------------------|---------------------|
| Sourcing, Production, Logistics | Shaping Customer Perceptions |
| Build "better" products | Tailor to consumption circumstances |
| "What else can we make and sell?" | "What else can we do for our customers?" |

**Key insight:** Fixed costs, customer value, and competitive advantage are shifting from inside-out to outside-in (Dawar 2013).

### Sources of Competitive Advantage

**Product Focus (Inside-Out):**
- Cost leadership
- Product differentiation
- Product/process innovation
- Operational effectiveness

**Customer Focus (Outside-In):**
- Define/shape customer purchase criteria
- Position in mind of customer
- Channel placement vs. competitive set
- Pricing strategy relative to competitive set

## Module 2: Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD)

### Core Principle

"The customer rarely buys what the business thinks it sells him." — Peter Drucker

- The **job**, not the customer, is the fundamental unit of analysis
- Same product may perform multiple jobs for different people
- Circumstances matter more than customer characteristics

### Three Ways to Find Jobs

1. **Identify problems** groups of people face (unmet goals, non-consumption, work-arounds, task avoidance)
2. **Combine existing solutions** — find coherent clusters of desires customers want to simultaneously satisfy
3. **Split existing solutions** — find segments within categories that particularly value certain goals

### Job-Market Analogy

| Employment Market | Product Market |
|-------------------|---------------|
| Job Candidates (skills, experience) | Products ("resume" of capabilities) |
| Employers (hiring needs) | Customers (jobs to be done) |
| "Common" qualifications | Baseline features |
| "Unique" qualifications | Differentiating features |
| "Don't do" | Features to avoid |

### Beware of Feature Creep

Adding features seems attractive but may improve products in ways irrelevant to customer needs. Ask: What needs will new features satisfy? Will existing customers be more satisfied? Will non-customers value it? Is it worth the cost?

### Traditional Segmentation vs. JTBD

**Traditional:** Demographic, geographic, psychographic, behavioral — often product/selling oriented, overemphasizes observables, stereotyping through personas.

**JTBD:** Segments based on what customers are trying to accomplish in specific circumstances — functional, social, and emotional dimensions.

### Key Case: Planters Nuts (JTBD analysis), Best Buy (customer centricity segmentation)

## Module 3: Customer Experience & Decision Journeys

### The Consumer Decision Journey (CDJ)

Replaces the traditional marketing funnel with a circular journey:

```
Need Recognition → Actively Considers → Searches & Learns → Evaluates & Likes
                                                                     ↓
Engages/Advocates ← Loyal Repeat ← Satisfied ← Consumes ← Plans to Buy ← WTP
```

### Touchpoints & Moments of Truth

Any interaction where a consumer contacts a brand — physical, digital, interpersonal, or indirect.

**Key insight:** Organizing by touchpoints reflects inside-out thinking. Customers don't care about internal silos — they want to get a job done.

### IMC Objectives, CDJ Stages & Media Types

| Communication Objective | CDJ Stage | Media Category |
|------------------------|-----------|---------------|
| Create awareness | Need recognition, Knows about brand | Paid (brand advertising, content marketing) |
| Convey information | Actively considers, Searches | Owned (websites, demos, newsletters) |
| Build trust | Willingness to pay | Earned (credible reviews, independent verification) |
| Inspire action | Plans to buy, Loyalty | Paid (promotions), Owned (discounts) |
| Instill loyalty | Satisfied, Repeat buyer | Owned (post-purchase support, loyalty programs) |
| Connect people | Engages, Advocates | Owned (community), Earned (word-of-mouth) |

### Journey Mapping vs. Empathy Mapping

- **Journey Mapping:** Maps customer path through touchpoints (can be inside-out)
- **Empathy Mapping:** User-focused approach to co-design; builds empathy, compassion, and first-person understanding — more consistent with JTBD and outside-in approaches

### Key Case: Jaguar Land Rover (customer experience transformation)

## Module 4: Data-Informed Marketing Strategy

### Design Thinking Process

| Phase | Activity |
|-------|----------|
| **Discover** | Learn about audience by observation and interview |
| **Explore** | Create point of view based on user needs and insights |
| **Map** | Brainstorm creative solutions |
| **Create** | Build prototypes to show others |
| **Test** | Share prototyped idea for feedback |

### Experimentation Framework

**Why experiment:** Forward-looking, data-informed approach. Goal: establish causation.

**Types of experiments:**
- Lab experiments (behavioral science labs, test kitchens)
- Field experiments (A/B testing in real-world environments)
- Test markets/pilot programs
- Natural experiments

### Basics of Experiments

| Component | Definition |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Hypothesis** | Testable statement about a causal relationship |
| **Dependent Variable (DV)** | Variable being measured (Y) |
| **Independent Variable (IV)** | Variable being changed (X) |
| **Treatment** | Change employed to produce effect |
| **Treatment/Control Groups** | Groups exposed/not exposed to treatment |

### Demonstrating Causation (Three Requirements)

1. **Concomitant Variation** — Statistical relationship between variables
2. **Appropriate Time Order** — IV change occurs before DV change
3. **Elimination of Other Factors** — Change not caused by confounds

### Validity & Reliability

- **Internal validity:** Extent competing explanations can be ruled out
- **External validity:** Extent causal relationships generalize to other contexts
- **Reliability:** Reproducibility of results

### HBR Guide to Smart Business Experiments

1. Focus on individuals, think short term
2. Keep it simple
3. Start with proof-of-concept test
4. Slice the data when results come in
5. Try out-of-the-box thinking
6. Measure everything that matters
7. Look for natural experiments

## Module 5: AI & MarTech Fundamentals

### AI Implementation Framework

For any AI use case, define:
1. **Data Inputs** — What data feeds the model (behavioral, metadata, images, etc.)
2. **Model Selection** — Classification, clustering, generative, or hybrid
3. **Real-Time or Batch** — When is processing needed
4. **Evaluation Metrics** — Managerial (CTR, conversion, LTV) and technical (accuracy, latency)
5. **Deployment Method** — Platform, triggers, integration points

### Use Case 1: Personalized Email

- **Inputs:** Behavioral history, user metadata, product metadata, campaign performance
- **Models:** Classification (targeting) + Text personalization (tone/style)
- **Deployment:** Batch for loyal segments, real-time triggers for new/at-risk segments
- **Improved goal:** Tone-personalized promos to matched segments via batch + real-time site triggers

### Use Case 2: Image-Based Trend Detection

- **Inputs:** Scraped/owned images, tags/captions, historical product images
- **Models:** CNNs/ViT, CLIP (image/text alignment), clustering (K-Means, UMAP+DBSCAN)
- **Deployment:** Batch (weekly/monthly), internal dashboards for design/product teams
- **Ethical concerns:** Real-time surveillance is definitely unethical and sometimes illegal

### AI Failures Taxonomy

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Flawed Tech** | Model learned the wrong thing | Wolf vs. dog (background, not animal) |
| **Flawed Human** | Right thing implemented wrong | YouTube optimizing for watch time → misinformation |
| **Total Disaster** | Wrong learning + wrong implementation | Biased hiring tools + rushed deployment |

## Market Sizing: Bottom-Up TAM/SAM/SOM

Rigorous market sizing is fundamental to any marketing strategy, new venture evaluation, or investor pitch. Two methods exist; the bottom-up approach is preferred for MBA-quality analysis because it forces testable assumptions.

### Definitions

| Term | Full Name | Definition |
|------|-----------|------------|
| **TAM** | Total Addressable Market | Total demand for your product/service if you captured 100% of the market |
| **SAM** | Serviceable Addressable Market | The segment of TAM you can realistically reach with your current business model, geography, and channels |
| **SOM** | Serviceable Obtainable Market | The portion of SAM you can realistically capture in the near term (1-3 years) |

### Top-Down Method (Quick Estimate)

1. Start with industry reports (Gartner, IDC, Statista, IBISWorld)
2. TAM = Total industry revenue for your category
3. SAM = TAM × % in your geographic/segment scope
4. SOM = SAM × realistic market share (typically 1-5% for new entrants)

**Limitation:** Relies on analyst estimates; doesn't validate whether customers actually want *your* specific solution.

### Bottom-Up Method (Preferred for MBA Analysis)

1. **Count target customers** in your specific segment (census data, LinkedIn Sales Navigator, industry directories)
2. **Estimate annual spend per customer** on your solution (survey data, competitor pricing, willingness-to-pay research)
3. **TAM = # target customers × annual spend per customer**
4. **SAM = TAM filtered by:** geography you can serve, channels you can access, product fit (which segments your product actually addresses)
5. **SOM = SAM × penetration rate** (based on comparable company benchmarks, go-to-market capacity)

### Worked Example: Premium Fitness App

```
Step 1: Target customers
  US adults who exercise regularly: 80M (CDC data)

Step 2: Annual spend
  Average fitness app spend: $120/year (Statista)
  Premium digital fitness spend: $180/year

Step 3: TAM
  TAM = 80M × $120/yr = $9.6B

Step 4: SAM
  Premium digital fitness users: 15M (subset who pay for premium apps)
  SAM = 15M × $180/yr = $2.7B

Step 5: SOM
  Year 1: 50K subscribers × $15/mo × 12 = $9M (0.3% of SAM)
  Year 3: 500K subscribers × $15/mo × 12 = $90M (3.3% of SAM)
```

### Validation Checks

- **Is SOM < 5% of SAM?** If higher, you need a compelling justification (unique advantage, viral loop, platform effects)
- **Does bottom-up align within 2x of top-down?** If not, revisit assumptions — one method has a flawed input
- **What's the implied customer acquisition rate?** Is it achievable given your CAC budget and go-to-market capacity?
- **Does the JTBD support the spend assumption?** Would a customer actually pay this much to solve this job?
