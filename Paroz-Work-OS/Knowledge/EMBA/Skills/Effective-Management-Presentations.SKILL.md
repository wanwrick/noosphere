## 2. Core Concepts

### 2.1 Executive/Leadership Presence

**Definition:** Your ability to inspire confidence so that:
- Senior leaders believe you have the potential for great achievements
- Peers believe you are capable and reliable
- Subordinates believe in you and want to follow you

**The Three Pillars of Executive Presence (2023 Research):**

| Pillar | Key Traits |
|--------|-----------|
| **Gravitas** | Confidence/grace under fire, Decisiveness, Inclusiveness, Vision/charisma, Integrity/courage, Respect |
| **Communication** | Superior speaking skills, Ability to command a room/Zoom, Listens and learns, Ability to read an audience, Telegraphs authenticity, Masters body language |
| **Appearance** | Polished, Authentic, Fit/vigorous, Dresses for new normal, Curates online image |

**Key Statistics:**
- Executive presence counts for **26%** of what it takes to be promoted (Center for Talent Innovation)
- **92%** of HR professionals believe executive presence is important for leadership
- **77%** believe those with high executive presence progress quickly
- In VC presentations, top predictors of success: Confidence, Comfort level, Passionate enthusiasm

### 2.2 The SUCCESs Principles (Made to Stick)

| Principle | Description | Application |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| **S - Simple** | If everything's important, nothing's important | Distill to one core message |
| **U - Unexpected** | Create demand for content before supplying it | Hook with surprise or curiosity |
| **C - Concrete** | Paint a mental picture | Provide specific examples |
| **C - Credible** | Provide necessary detail | Cite credible sources, offer "try before you buy" |
| **E - Emotional** | Connect with what audiences care about | Appeal to human quality |
| **S - Stories** | Help audience try on your information | Use narrative structure |

### 2.3 The Big Idea

Your Big Idea must:
1. Include your **unique point of view** on a topic
2. State **what's at stake** for those who adopt or don't adopt your point of view
3. Be written in the form of **one or two sentences**
4. Be **memorable and repeatable**

**Formula:**
> "I believe [unique perspective] because [evidence/reason], and if we don't act, [consequence], but if we do, [benefit]."

### 2.4 Content Selection: The 3×3 Template

Structure your presentation middle with:
- **3 Key Messages** (aligned to your Big Idea)
- **3 Supporting Points** per message (proof points, facts, examples)

**Selection Criteria:**
- Is it directly aligned to my Big Idea?
- Is it most important and relevant to THIS audience?
- Can it be visualized or made concrete?

## 3. Frameworks & Methodologies

### 3.1 RAMP Opening Framework

**R - Rapport Builder**
- Greet the audience warmly
- Tell them how much you look forward to being with them
- Thank host(s) and other distinguished members of the audience

**A - Attention Getter**
Options include:
- A provocative question
- A compelling story
- Startling statistics
- A memorable quote
- A demonstration

**M - Message**
- State your presentation's purpose clearly
- Answer: "What will the audience gain?" (WIIFM - What's In It For Me)

**P - Plan**
- Preview how many points you will cover
- State whether/when you will take questions
- Indicate the presentation length

### 3.2 Presentation Close Techniques

Your close should:
- Remind audience of the relationship between your topic and them
- Be brief yet memorable
- Reinforce the benefits of your message
- **NOT introduce new information**

**Close Options:**
1. Summarize main points
2. Quote someone relevant
3. Tell a story
4. Use poetic language
5. Call for action
6. Refer back to your opening (tie the bow)

### 3.3 The Motivated Sequence (Persuasive Presentations)

```
┌─────────────────┐
│  RAMP Opening   │
└────────┬────────┘
         ↓
┌─────────────────┐
│ 1. PROBLEM      │ → State problem, illustrate with examples,
│                 │   show how listener is directly affected
└────────┬────────┘
         ↓
┌─────────────────┐
│ 2. SOLUTION     │ → State solution, explain how it works,
│                 │   cite practical experience, address objections
└────────┬────────┘
         ↓
┌─────────────────┐
│ 3. VISUALIZATION│ → Choose one method:
│                 │   • Positive (wonderful if adopted)
│                 │   • Negative (terrible if not adopted)
│                 │   • Contrast (compare positive vs. negative)
└────────┬────────┘
         ↓
┌─────────────────┐
│ 4. ACTION       │ → Be precise and specific
│                 │   Include timeline
└────────┬────────┘
         ↓
┌─────────────────┐
│     CLOSE       │ → Reinforce WIIFM, end memorably
└─────────────────┘
```

### 3.4 Vocal Presence Frameworks

#### BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front)
1. **Lead with key message** (decision, request, or recommendation)
2. **Support with reasons**: 2-3 supporting arguments or facts
3. **Offer next steps** (if necessary): What action or decision is needed?

**Example:**
- ❌ "We've been reviewing market trends and have noted a slowdown in our primary market. There are different options to look at, we have explored some different ideas, and we have concluded that we should revamp our strategy to focus on a new demographic."
- ✅ "We should pivot our strategy to focus on a new demographic because the current market is slowing down."

#### PREP (Point, Reason, Example, Point)
| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| **P - Point** | State your main point or conclusion upfront |
| **R - Reason** | Provide a reason or rationale to back up your point |
| **E - Example** | Use a specific example or data point |
| **P - Point** | Restate your main point to reinforce your message |

**Example:**
- **Point**: "We should increase investment in our digital transformation."
- **Reason**: "Because our competitors are rapidly adopting new technologies."
- **Example**: "Company X invested in digital, which increased their market share by 10% in the last year."
- **Point**: "This is why I believe increasing our digital investment is critical for us."

#### SBI (Situation, Behavior, Impact)
Use for feedback - focuses on facts rather than emotions.

| Step | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **S - Situation** | Describe the situation in specific terms (where, when) |
| **B - Behavior** | Describe the behavior observed, without interpretation or judgment |
| **I - Impact** | Explain the impact of the behavior on the project, team, or business |

**Example (Constructive):**
- **Situation**: "During Monday's team meeting when we were reviewing the project timeline..."
- **Behavior**: "...you interrupted a few team members while they were speaking and dismissed some of their suggestions without explanation."
- **Impact**: "That made it difficult for others to contribute and may have discouraged open discussion. It also created some tension in the group."

### 3.5 Three Levels of Listening

| Level | Focus | Usage |
|-------|-------|-------|
| **Level 1: Internal/Self-Listening** | Your own thoughts, opinions, reactions | Brief self-awareness checks only |
| **Level 2: Focused/Active Listening** | Fully on the other person—words, tone, emotions, meaning | Building trust, coaching, feedback, one-on-one communication |
| **Level 3: Global Listening** | Tone, energy, body language, environment, what's NOT said | Strategic leadership, navigating complex dynamics, reading room temperature |

**Best Practice:** Shift fluidly between all three levels
- Use Level 1 briefly to check in with your own reactions
- Operate mostly in Level 2 during conversations
- Tap into Level 3 when managing a room, sensing team morale, or leading change

## 4. Presentation Types & Requirements

### 4.1 Baseline Presentation (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Inform or persuade with visual support

**Requirements:**
- 5-minute time limit
- Must include visual aids/slides
- Can be adapted from a real work presentation
- Delivered twice (initial + redo with improvements)

**Structure:**
- RAMP opening (~30 seconds)
- 3×3 body content (~4 minutes)
- Memorable close (~30 seconds)

**Preparation Checklist:**
- [ ] Big Idea defined (one sentence)
- [ ] 3 key messages identified
- [ ] 3 supporting points per message
- [ ] Visual slides created (pass 3-second glance test)
- [ ] Practice with timer
- [ ] Q&A scenarios prepared

### 4.2 Impromptu Presentation (1-2 minutes)

**Purpose:** Present information clearly with no preparation time

**Best Approach:**
- Use PREP or BLUF framework
- Focus on ONE main point
- Support with specific example
- End with clear takeaway

**Success Tips:**
- Take a breath before starting
- Lead with the bottom line (BLUF)
- Draw from personal experience
- End definitively (don't trail off)

### 4.3 Persuasive Speech (2 minutes)

**Purpose:** Establish thought leadership; demonstrate executive presence

**Topics (Choose One):**
- The biggest challenge(s) facing your industry today
- The biggest trend(s) in your profession/field of expertise

**Requirements:**
- 2-minute time limit
- NO slides (pure executive presence)
- Standing position
- Command of room/Zoom
- Handle challenging Q&A

**Success Criteria:**
- Audience generally agrees with your viewpoint
- Audience recognizes you as a leader in your field

**Structure:**
- Hook with problem (~20 seconds)
- Present solution (~40 seconds)
- Visualize outcome (~40 seconds)
- Call to action (~20 seconds)

## 5. Visual Design Principles

### 5.1 The 3-Second Rule (Glance Test)

Every slide must pass the glance test:
- Main message clear in 3 seconds
- Audience should "get it" immediately

### 5.2 Visual Slide Commandments

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Keep slides simple | Crowd slides with text |
| Use one idea per slide | Put multiple concepts on one slide |
| More visuals, less text | Create word-heavy slides |
| Support your message | Compete with your slides |
| Face your audience | Talk to your slides |
| Use builds sparingly | Overuse animations |

### 5.3 Slides vs. Slidedocs

| Type | Purpose | Text Density |
|------|---------|--------------|
| **Presentation Slides** | Visual support while presenting | Minimal (headlines, key visuals) |
| **Slidedocs** | Documents designed to be read | Dense (can stand alone without presenter) |

### 5.4 Slide Quantity Guidelines

| Slides per Minute | Use Case |
|-------------------|----------|
| 0 slides | Logistics, introductions, panels |
| 1-2 slides/minute | Workshops, detailed content |
| ~5 slides/minute | Keynotes, rapid-fire style |
| 20+ slides/minute | Self-navigated decks |

## 6. Q&A Mastery: The 10-Point System

| # | Strategy | Implementation |
|---|----------|----------------|
| 1 | **State the Rules** | Tell audience when/how you'll take questions; point out microphones; state rules clearly |
| 2 | **Prepare** | List and practice all possible questions, especially the worst ones |
| 3 | **Maintain Control** | Formally recognize questioners; limit number of questions; allow only one person to speak at a time |
| 4 | **Include the Group** | Move away from questioner while listening; paraphrase question for group; answer to the whole room |
| 5 | **Ask Your Question First** | "A question I'm often asked is..." then answer to prime the pump |
| 6 | **Admit You Don't Know** | "I don't know but I'll get back to you" (max 2 times) |
| 7 | **Leverage Expertise** | "Are there experts in the audience who can add to this?" |
| 8 | **Acknowledge Dissenters** | "Thank you for your opinion, I know there are different schools of thought—this is what has worked for me" |
| 9 | **Avoid Sincerity Traps** | Don't say "That's a good question" after every question |
| 10 | **End Strong** | Don't end on questions; always finish with a closing statement that reinforces your message |

**Bonus Tip:** Plant the question you most want to hear—ask someone to pose it on your signal.

### Question Types to Prepare For:

| Type | Example |
|------|---------|
| **Clarification** | "Can you explain what you mean by X?" |
| **Challenge** | "Why should this be priority over Y?" |
| **Evidence** | "Do you have stats that support this?" |
| **Impact** | "What happens if we fail?" |
| **Skeptical** | "C-suite won't go for that. How will you win them over?" |

## 7. Leadership Presence Toolkit

### 7.1 Physical Presence

**Do:**
- Carry yourself in a positive light; show confidence
- Use open body language
- Use purposeful gestures, avoiding nervous habits
- Keep a calm, neutral facial expression, even during tense moments
- Stand with confidence
- Make deliberate movements

**Don't:**
- Lean back or slouch
- Use closed gestures / locked arms and hands
- Touch your face
- Avoid eye contact
- Make yourself small
- Nod excessively

### 7.2 Vocal Presence

#### What You Say
Avoid weak language:
- ❌ Seeking validation tags ("you know?" "Don't you agree?" "Isn't that so?")
- ❌ Overuse of modifiers/qualifiers ("only," "just," "a bit," "quite," "rather," "pretty much," "perhaps," "maybe," "sort of")
- ❌ Hedges ("I feel like," "in my opinion," "I would like to," "I might be wrong, but...")
- ❌ Unnecessary apologies
- ❌ Self-deprecation ("I'm no expert but...")

#### How You Say It
- Speak in a measured, deliberate way
- Speak with conviction
- Project your voice without shouting
- Aim for a calm, steady volume
- Use pauses strategically to give weight to your points

### 7.3 Mental Presence

- Practice active listening—don't interrupt; give full attention
- Stay focused on the topic at hand
- Avoid being distracted or appearing rushed
- Refrain from emotional reactions, even if challenged
- Stay composed under pressure

## 8. Storytelling Mastery

### 8.1 Why Stories Matter

| Metric | Stories |
|--------|---------|
| Data recall | 5% |
| Story recall | 63% |

**Stories:**
- Inspire and motivate
- Unite people
- Simplify complex messages
- Solidify abstract concepts

### 8.2 Story Types (Duarte Framework)

**Warning Stories:** Use when audience is stuck or heading toward wrong direction

**Motivating Stories:** Use when audience is energized and ready for action
- Heed the call
- Seek the reward
- Overcome the enemy
- Endure the struggle
- Savor the win

### 8.3 Three-Act Story Structure

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ ACT 1: SITUATION (Beginning)                     │
│ → Set the scene                                  │
│ → Introduce characters and circumstances         │
└────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────┘
                     ↓
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ ACT 2: COMPLICATION (Messy Middle)               │
│ → Present the conflict                           │
│ → Raise the stakes                               │
│ → Hero confronts adversarial force               │
└────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────┘
                     ↓
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ ACT 3: RESOLUTION (End)                          │
│ → Show the outcome                               │
│ → Connect to your Big Idea                       │
│ → Hero resolves conflict                         │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 8.4 Data Storytelling: Three Acts

| Act | Focus | Example (Opportunity) | Example (Problem) |
|-----|-------|----------------------|-------------------|
| **Situation** | Problem or opportunity in the data | Our pilot of onsite college recruiting was well attended | Contract stipulates we cover travel costs |
| **Complication** | Data presents issues or opportunities | Entry-level candidates are 28% more likely to accept if they meet us at a fair | International travel costs rose 5% last Q; profits down to 2% |
| **Resolution** | Your big idea addresses problem with positive outcome | Extend campus program to five more universities | Re-negotiate contract re: travel |

### 8.5 Making Data Relatable

- **Attach data to something relatable** (familiar scale references)
- **Develop a sense of scale** (size, distance, time, speed)
- **Connect to audience experience**
- **Visualize the impact**

## 10. Peer Review Criteria

### Baseline Presentation Evaluation (Scale: 1-5)

**Content:**
- Clear statement of purpose
- Effective opening
- Organized middle
- Compelling close

**Delivery:**
- Command of the material
- Visual aids
- Eye contact / Facial expressions
- Body language / Gestures
- Voice qualities

### Persuasive Speech Evaluation

**Observe:**
- Physical presence
- Vocal presence
- Mental presence
- Overall executive presence / confidence (1-5 scale)

**Provide:**
- What you liked about the speech
- Something to improve / what could have been more effective

## 11. Self-Reflection Framework

### Categories to Evaluate (Scale 1-4)

| Category | Elements |
|----------|----------|
| **Preparation** | Found/prepared relevant information; Rehearsed; Used feedback to improve |
| **Presentation Structure** | Clear intro/purpose; Outlined key points; Organized information; Included conclusion |

## 13. Virtual Presentation Additions

### The 6 Sins of Webcam Presentations (Stand & Deliver)

| Sin | Fix |
|-----|-----|
| Poor camera position | Camera at eye level |
| Bad background | Professional, uncluttered background |
| Poor lighting | Light your face (not behind you) |
| Audio issues | Test audio; use quality microphone |
| Distractions | Minimize notifications; close unnecessary apps |
| Looking at screen | Look at camera lens, not screen |

### Virtual Presence Tips
- Increase energy by ~20%
- Use more facial expressions (they get compressed on video)
- Gesture within camera frame
- Stand if possible for important presentations
