## The Two Systems of Thinking

| System 1: FAST | System 2: SLOW |
|----------------|----------------|
| Intuition | Critical Thinking |
| Pattern recognition | Deliberate analysis |
| Immediate understanding | Conscious reasoning |
| Good for low-stakes, familiar situations | Required for moderate-to-high-stakes problems |

**Key insight:** Not every issue requires critical thinking. Calibrate decision stakes first.

### Building Intuition (System 1)

Anyone can become more intuitive by paying closer attention to experiences.

**After-Action Review:**
1. What was supposed to happen? (What were we trying to accomplish?)
2. What actually happened? (How close did we come?)
3. What went well? Why?
4. What did not go well? Why?
5. What do we want to keep doing?
6. What do we want to do differently next time?

## Decision Stakes Calibration: 10-10-10

Before engaging System 2 thinking, ask:

**Will this decision truly matter:**
- 10 **hours** (or days) from now?
- 10 **weeks** (or months) from now?
- 10 **YEARS** from now?

**Follow-up coaching questions:**
1. What's the worst case scenario? How likely is it (1-10)?
2. What's the best case scenario? How possible is it (1-10)?
3. How reversible is this decision?

**Decision rule:** If best case is even a little possible, worst case isn't that bad/likely, and decision is fairly reversible → just decide and act.

## Three-Question Information Sorting Tool

"Reducing the Unknown Unknowns"

| Step | Question | Purpose |
|------|----------|---------|
| 1. **KNOW** | What do we KNOW, and HOW do we know it? | Establish factual foundation |
| 2. **ASSUME** | What are we ASSUMING, and are those assumptions necessarily valid? | Challenge hidden assumptions |
| 3. **DON'T KNOW** | What information are we MISSING that might be relevant? | Identify knowledge gaps |

**Leadership signal:** Asking "What do WE know?" signals: collective responsibility, need for everyone's input, leader doesn't claim to know everything, different perspectives are valued.

## The Context + IDEALS Problem-Solving Method

### The Seven Steps

```
         CONTEXT + "IDEALS"

                              IDENTIFY PROBLEM
           1
SELF-CORRECT  6           2  DETERMINE ROOT CAUSES
LIST REASONS     CONTEXT
   WHY        5           3  ENUMERATE OPTIONS
      ASSESS BEST
           OPTION
              4
```

### Step 1: Assess the Context

#### Macro Context (PESTLE)

| Factor | Key Question |
|--------|-------------|
| **P**olitical | Government influence on industry? Political stability? |
| **E**conomic | Economy growing/shrinking? Wages, unemployment, interest rates? |
| **S**ocial | Demographic trends? Lifestyle shifts? Consumer preferences? |
| **T**echnological | New technologies emerging? Adoption rates? Impact on processes? |
| **L**egal | How regulated? Recent regulatory changes? |
| **E**nvironmental | Climate impact on industry? Operational risks/opportunities? |

#### Micro Context

| Element | Questions |
|---------|----------|
| **When** | What year(s)? Macro timing significance? Internal timing issues? |
| **Where** | Location and its significance? |
| **Company** | Type/industry? Business model? Strategy? Current financial state? Recent events? |
| **Decision-Maker** | Key attributes? Blind spots? Success criteria? |
| **Other Actors** | Expertise, roles, values, prior beliefs about the issues? |
| **Your Biases** | Personal values/beliefs/preferences? Past experiences? Steps to neutralize? |

**Key rule:** "Context is Queen." When analyzing significance of a context fact, ask: "If this fact were different (or not present), how might that change our analysis?"

### Step 2: Identify Problems (IDEALS — I)

- **Immediate Problem:** What actions should the decision-maker take regarding Current State to achieve Desired State? (Frame as a question)
- **Bigger Picture Problem:** The overarching/systemic issue of which the immediate problem is a symptom (address root causes)

### Step 3: Determine Root Causes (IDEALS — D)

Use the **Congruence Model** to identify the three most significant root causes creating the gap between Current State and Desired State. Each root cause needs supporting evidence from the case.

**Congruence Model Elements (from Managing & Leading Orgs, Prof. Mannix):**
| Element | Questions |
|---------|-----------|
| **Task** | What is the core work? What are the workflows, interdependencies? |
| **People** | Who does the work? Skills, preferences, demographics? |
| **Formal Organization** | Structure, systems, processes, policies, rewards? |
| **Informal Organization** | Culture, norms, power dynamics, unwritten rules? |

Root causes arise from **misalignment between elements**. For each root cause, quantify impact where possible:

| Root Cause | Evidence | Impact ($/%, if estimable) | Severity (1-5) |
|-----------|----------|---------------------------|----------------|
| RC1: [name] | [case facts] | [e.g., $X revenue at risk, Y% productivity loss] | [1-5] |
| RC2: [name] | [case facts] | [estimated financial/operational impact] | [1-5] |
| RC3: [name] | [case facts] | [estimated financial/operational impact] | [1-5] |

### Step 4: Enumerate Options for Solving (IDEALS — E)

Brainstorm **three alternative solutions** for the Immediate Problem. For each:
- How does it solve the problem (bridge the gap)?
- What constraints exist?
- How can the organization manage those constraints?

### Step 5: Assess Best Option (IDEALS — A)

Evaluate which alternative is both:
- **Desirable:** Meets decision-maker's criteria, advances strategic priorities, manifests company values
- **Feasible:** Considers constraints of time, money, people, infrastructure, legal/ethical, strategic priorities, organizational politics

### Step 6: List Reasons Why (IDEALS — L)

Explain specifically why the recommended solution is superior to the rejected alternatives. Compare on: likelihood of achieving Desired State, strategic alignment, organizational values, constraint manageability.

### Step 7: Self-Correct (IDEALS — S)

- Who will object to the proposed solution and why?
- How should the decision-maker respond to these objections?
- What are the key risks? How can they be mitigated?
- Why should the company proceed despite risks? (Benefits outweigh risks)

## Executive Summary Structure

The 3-part test: **Neutrality. Clarity. Specificity.**

1. **Situation** — Neutrally describe facts that led to the analysis (no editorializing)
2. **Problems/Questions** — Clearly summarize immediate and bigger picture problems
3. **Recommended Solutions** — Specific enough that audience understands proposed actions without reading full analysis

---

## Communicating the Analysis

1. Briefly describe the **situation** that gave rise to the problem
2. What **complication** led to the analysis? (Problem to solve, opportunity to seize)
3. What is your **analysis**? (Causes, proposed solution)
4. How specifically does your recommended plan **solve the problem**?
5. Why is your analysis **optimal**? (More benefits, fewer risks, more implementable than alternatives)
