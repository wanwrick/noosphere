## 2. Core Concepts

### 2.1 Management vs. Leadership (Kotter Framework)

| Dimension | Management | Leadership |
|-----------|------------|------------|
| **Purpose** | Coping with complexity | Coping with change |
| **Direction** | Planning and budgeting | Setting direction (vision) |
| **People** | Organizing and staffing | Aligning people |
| **Execution** | Controlling and problem-solving | Motivating and inspiring |
| **Orientation** | Orderly results, predictability | Change, transformation |

**Key Insight**: "Most U.S. corporations today are overmanaged and underled." The real challenge is combining strong leadership and strong management.

### 2.2 Managerial Roles (Mintzberg's 10 Roles)

**Interpersonal Roles** (from formal authority):
1. **Figurehead**: Ceremonial duties, representing the organization
2. **Leader**: Motivating, training, hiring staff
3. **Liaison**: Building external networks and contacts

**Informational Roles** (from interpersonal contacts):
4. **Monitor**: Scanning environment for information
5. **Disseminator**: Passing privileged information to subordinates
6. **Spokesperson**: Communicating to external parties

**Decisional Roles** (from information access):
7. **Entrepreneur**: Initiating improvement projects
8. **Disturbance Handler**: Responding to unexpected pressures
9. **Resource Allocator**: Deciding who gets what (including own time)
10. **Negotiator**: Committing organizational resources in real-time

**Reality Check**: Managers work at an unrelenting pace with brevity, variety, and discontinuity. Half of activities last less than 9 minutes. Only 1% of time is spent in open-ended observational tours.

### 2.3 The Ten Operational Duties of General Managers

From Murray/Richardson research on ~200 General Managers:

1. **Meeting budget and performance targets** - Prime operational concern
2. **Running meetings, chairing committees** - Periodic and ad hoc
3. **Setting the tone: Personal HR tasks** - Walking the talk on values
4. **Unplanned firefighting/problem-solving** - Significant portion of workday
5. **Ensuring operations meet targets** - Daily/weekly indicator reviews
6. **Leading internal communications** - Hub for alignment across functions
7. **Supporting sales and marketing** - Key account relationship management
8. **Managing the Board/boss** - Earning license to operate
9. **External stakeholder representation** - Compliance, regulation, industry
10. **Building community relations** - License to operate, CSR

**Warning**: Career paths create "blind spots" - areas where managers lack skills and avoid engagement. Effective GMs must engage in ALL ten duties.

### 2.4 Information Processing Reality

- Managers spend 66-80% of time in verbal (oral) communication
- Strategic data bank is in managers' minds, not computers
- "Today's gossip may be tomorrow's fact" - soft information drives decisions
- **Dilemma of delegation**: Can't hand over a dossier; must "dump memory"

| Internal | External |

**Part 1: What We Do**
- For the [target market]
- Who want the [consumption problem solved]

## 4. Meeting Management

### 4.1 Calendar of Meetings Framework

| Meeting Type | Frequency | Purpose |
|--------------|-----------|---------|
| Leadership retreat | 3-4 days/year | Strategy/teambuilding |
| Strategy update | 1-2 days/quarter | Updating strategic direction |
| Strategy discussion | 1 hour/month | Strategic alignment |
| Operational team meeting | Weekly or monthly (1-2 hours) | Operational coordination |
| One-on-one coaching | Weekly or monthly | Direct report development |
| All-hands communication | Quarterly | Transparency with all employees |

### 4.2 Running a Great Meeting (Richardson)

**Before the Meeting**:
- Always have an agenda with start/finish time and clear objectives
- Only invite people who really need to be there
- Ask attendees to prepare/read required materials beforehand
- Encourage arrival 2-3 minutes early

**During the Meeting**:
- Start with brief review of objectives and agenda
- Encourage process checks if discussion goes off-topic
- Ask for brief comments from everyone on disagreements
- Periodically summarize discussion and what remains
- Attempt to end on time; allow exits if overrunning

**Ending the Meeting**:
- Develop recommendations and next-step action plans
- Make What, Who, and When clear (creates accountability)

**After the Meeting**:
- Issue one-page bulleted summary
- Include key discussion points, recommendations, action plans
- Distribute to those who need to be informed (not just attendees)
- Do NOT ask non-attendees for proposed revisions

### 4.3 "Managing Me" Leadership Principles (Shyloski Model)

Core expectations for direct reports:

1. **Safety passion** - Show demonstrated commitment
2. **Answer questions** - Short, to the point
3. **No bad questions/ideas/decisions** - Only those never asked/surfaced/made
4. **No surprises** - Bad news gets worse with age
5. **Follow through on commitments** - Your word is your bond
6. **Never chastise for answering questions** - Take issues to the leader
7. **Apologize when losing temper** - It's a crime not to
8. **Go slow to go fast** - Plan, get commitment, follow through
9. **Clarify FYI vs. direction needed** - Or expect direction regardless
10. **What and When first** - Why and How only if asked
11. **"We" not "they"** - Team mentality
12. **Healthy skepticism, not cynicism** - Cynicism destroys morale
13. **Strong, open criticism welcome** - Makes us stronger
14. **24/7 availability** - Don't apologize for calling
15. **Construction work is challenging** - Embrace the dynamism

## 5. Agile User Story Framework

### 5.1 User Story Template

```markdown
## Title
A concise, descriptive name for the story

## Desired Business Outcome
[action] to [result] [by/for/to/of] a(n)/the [object] so that [business value is achieved]

## Acceptance Criteria
- Standard path and any deviations
- Precise, measurable outcomes

## Technical Details
Specifies how to implement the solution

## Testing Scenarios
- Step-by-step test cases as sub-bullets
```

### 5.2 Story Writing Principles

- **Business-driven**: Understandable to stakeholders, focused on outcomes
- **Specific**: Acceptance criteria are precise, unambiguous, testable
- **Atomic**: Subtasks are discrete units with clear inputs/outputs
- **Iterative**: Continuously refine with team collaboration
- **Test-driven**: Comprehensive test cases developed upfront

Expected deliverable or result

## Notes
Helpful implementation guidance

**Executive Summary Structure**:
1. The Business Decision (3 key points)
2. Market Compensation Analysis
3. Documented Value Contribution (quantified)
4. Retention Risk Assessment
5. Critical Dependencies
6. Investment Analysis (ROI)
7. Recommendation and Implementation

### 7.3 Value Contribution Quantification

**Categories to Document**:
- Automation savings (hours × loaded hourly rate)
- Framework/reuse value (dev hours saved × deployments)
- Production stability (incidents prevented × cost per incident)
- Operational efficiency (time savings, cost reductions)

**Calculation Example**:
```
Annual Savings = Hours Saved × (Base Salary ÷ 2,080) × 1.4 overhead
```

### 7.4 Retention Risk Factors

| Factor | Impact Level | Indicator |
|--------|--------------|-----------|
| Compensation disparity | High | % below market |
| Title-responsibility mismatch | Medium | Senior work, intermediate title |
| Market demand | High | Recruiter contacts/month |
| Internal inequity | Critical | Gap vs. recent hires |

### 7.5 Replacement Cost Formula

**Industry Standard**: 150% of annual salary

**Components**:
- Direct recruitment costs (fees, interview time, admin)
- Productivity loss (onboarding, ramp-up, knowledge transfer)
- Business disruption (delays, relationship rebuilding, team impact)

### 7.6 ROI Calculation

```
ROI = (Replacement Cost Avoided - Annual Investment) ÷ Annual Investment × 100

Investment = Salary Increase + Benefits Overhead (typically 20%)
Benefit = Replacement Cost × Retention Probability
```
