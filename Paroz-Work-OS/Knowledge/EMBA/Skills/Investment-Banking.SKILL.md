# 2. CORE CONCEPTS

## 2.1 Investment Banking Fundamentals

**Definition**: Investment banking involves advising on and facilitating large financial transactions, primarily M&A (mergers and acquisitions) and corporate financing, with a focus on transaction structuring, valuation, and deal mechanics.

**Key Transaction Types**:
1. **Mergers & Acquisitions (M&A)**: One company acquiring another; buyer assumes target's operations and liabilities
2. **Leveraged Buyouts (LBOs)**: Acquisition funded primarily with debt; sponsor (buyer) uses target's cash flows to service debt
3. **Divestitures**: Selling subsidiary or business unit
4. **Recapitalizations**: Refinancing existing debt or adding leverage to mature company

**Sponsor's Core Objective in LBO**: Maximize equity return (IRR and MOIC) by structuring leverage appropriately, achieving operational improvements, and executing a profitable exit.

## 2.2 Leveraged Buyout (LBO) Mechanics

### The Basic LBO Structure

An LBO involves purchasing a company primarily with borrowed money, with the target company's cash flows serving as the primary source of debt repayment.

**Sources & Uses Statement** (Initial Transaction):
```
SOURCES:
  Equity (Sponsor's cash investment)         $XXX
  Senior Secured Debt (Bank loans)           $XXX
  Mezzanine Debt (Subordinated)              $XXX
  Total Sources                              $XXX

USES:
  Purchase Price (Fair Value of Target)      $XXX
  Transaction Fees & Expenses                $XXX
  Debt Fees                                  $XXX
  Working Capital Adjustment                 $XXX
  Total Uses                                 $XXX
```

**Key Principle**: Sources must equal Uses. This ensures proper accounting of all cash flows.

### Cash Flow Dynamics in an LBO

The LBO model projects forward the target's financial statements under new ownership:

1. **Operating Cash Flow** = Cash generated by the business after maintaining/growing it
2. **Debt Service** = Principal + Interest payments (uses cash)
3. **Excess Cash Flow** = Operating cash flow - Debt service - CapEx - Working capital changes
4. **Debt Paydown** = Available excess cash goes to reduce debt outstanding
5. **Exit** = At year 5-7, sponsor sells company; uses proceeds to pay off remaining debt; keeps equity profit

### LBO Returns Framework

**Internal Rate of Return (IRR)**: The annualized return to equity investors
```
IRR Definition: The discount rate that makes NPV of all equity cash flows = 0
- Initial equity investment = Negative cash flow (Year 0)
- Interim equity distributions = Positive cash flows
- Exit equity proceeds = Final positive cash flow

IRR = Discount rate where: Initial Investment + PV(Distributions) + PV(Exit Proceeds) = 0
```

**Multiple on Invested Capital (MOIC)**:
```
MOIC = (Distributions + Exit Equity Proceeds) / Initial Equity Investment
```

**Relationship**: MOIC and IRR both measure equity return; IRR assumes reinvestment at IRR rate; MOIC is cumulative return multiple.

**Typical Target Returns in LBOs**:
- Lower market cap, stable businesses: 20-25% IRR target
- Mid-market: 25-30% IRR target
- Small companies, high growth: 30-40%+ IRR target

### Sources of LBO Returns (The Equation)

Sponsor returns come from four sources:

1. **Leverage/Multiple Expansion Effect**:
   - Sponsor buys at 8x EBITDA
   - Leverages 5-6x debt
   - Company grows modestly
   - Sponsor exits at 9-10x EBITDA (multiple expansion)
   - Benefit = Higher exit multiple on growing base

2. **Debt Paydown**:
   - Company generates FCF
   - FCF used to reduce debt outstanding
   - Lower debt → higher equity value at exit
   - Benefit = Equity captures debt paydown + growth

3. **Growth/EBITDA Improvement**:
   - Operational improvements
   - Cost cutting
   - Revenue acceleration
   - Synergies (if acquired company)
   - Benefit = Higher exit enterprise value

4. **Timing/Market Conditions**:
   - Exit at favorable market multiples
   - Refinancing to better terms
   - Interest rate environment

**The Three-Statement Integration**:

An LBO model links three financial statements:

| Statement | Role | Key Linkages |
|-----------|------|--------------|
| **P&L** | Operating profit | Revenue - COGS - OpEx = EBITDA - D&A = EBIT - Interest = Net Income |
| **Cash Flow** | Available cash to service debt | Operating CF - CapEx - Δ Working Cap = Free Cash Flow |
| **Balance Sheet** | Debt and equity balances | Uses of CF: Debt paydown, distributions, working capital; Financing: Debt and equity value |

## 2.3 Debt Structure and Coverage Ratios

### Debt Stack Hierarchy

In an LBO, multiple layers of debt have different seniority and pricing:

1. **Senior Secured Debt** (Lowest cost, ~LIBOR + 300-400 bps)
   - First lien on company assets
   - Lowest interest rate
   - Most restrictive covenants
   - Banks' typical hold

2. **Mezzanine Debt** (Mid-level, ~15-18% yield)
   - Second lien position
   - Hybrid debt-equity characteristics
   - Equity kickers (warrants)
   - Asset-based lending or subordinated

3. **Equity/Sponsor Capital** (Residual)
   - Last to receive proceeds in default
   - Highest return expectation

### Debt Capacity & Leverage Metrics

**Total Leverage**: Measures how much total debt the company has relative to operating cash flow
```
Total Leverage Ratio = Total Debt / EBITDA
```
- Higher ratio = more aggressive leverage (higher risk, higher return target)
- Typical LBO range: 4.0x - 6.0x (conservative to aggressive)
- Cyclical businesses: 3.0x - 4.5x (lower to protect through downturns)
- Stable/growing: 4.5x - 6.5x (higher acceptable)

**Senior Leverage**: Only bank debt / EBITDA
```
Senior Leverage = Senior Debt / EBITDA
```
- Typical: 2.5x - 3.5x
- Senior lenders care about ability to be repaid from cash flow

### Coverage Ratios (Debt Service Capability)

**Interest Coverage**: Can company pay interest from operating earnings?
```
Interest Coverage = EBITDA / Interest Expense
```
- Healthy range: >3.0x (EBITDA covers interest 3x over)
- Below 2.0x: Risk of covenant violations
- Used by lenders to assess annual interest payment capacity

**Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR)**: Can company pay all debt obligations?
```
DSCR = Free Cash Flow / (Interest + Principal Payments)
```
- >1.2x: Comfortable coverage
- <1.0x: Negative cash flow; must use other sources to service debt
- Critical in structured debt documents

**Leverage Reduction Schedule**:
```
Year 1 Total Debt: $500M
Year 2 Total Debt: $450M (after $50M paydown)
Year 3 Total Debt: $390M (after $60M paydown)
...
Exit (Year 5) Debt: $250M (40% of original)
```

Higher paydown = lower exit debt = higher equity return.

## 2.4 M&A Accretion/Dilution Analysis

### The Core Question

When Company A acquires Company B, how does the deal affect Company A's **earnings per share (EPS)**?

**Definition**:
- **Accretive Deal**: Target's contribution to EPS exceeds cost to buyer → EPS increases
- **Dilutive Deal**: Target's contribution is less than cost → EPS decreases
- **Neutral**: No change to EPS

### The Accretion/Dilution Waterfall

**Step 1: Measure Target's Contribution**
```
Target's Operating Income (EBIT) × (1 - Tax Rate) = Net Income Contribution
```

**Step 2: Calculate Incremental Debt Service**
```
If buyer finances acquisition with debt:
  Debt raised = Purchase Price
  Annual Interest = Debt × Interest Rate
  After-tax interest cost = Interest × (1 - Tax Rate)
```

**Step 3: Calculate Accretion/(Dilution)**
```
Target's Net Income Contribution           = $X
Less: After-tax Debt Service on Financing  = $(Y)
Net Accretion/(Dilution)                   = $X - $Y
```

**Step 4: Per Share Impact**
```
Accretion/(Dilution) per share = Net Accretion/(Dilution) / Buyer's Shares Outstanding
EPS Impact % = Accretion/(Dilution) per share / Buyer's Current EPS
```

### Example: Accretion/Dilution Calculation

**Assumptions**:
- Buyer: Current EPS = $2.00, Shares = 100M
- Target: EBIT = $50M, Tax Rate = 25%
- Purchase Price: $500M, financed 100% with debt at 5% rate

**Calculation**:
```
Target Contribution:
  EBIT                                    $50M
  Tax (25%)                               ($12.5M)
  Net Income                              $37.5M

Buyer's Financing Cost:
  Debt                                    $500M
  Interest Rate                           5%
  Annual Interest                         $25M
  Tax Benefit (25%)                       ($6.25M)
  After-tax Interest Cost                 $18.75M

Net Accretion:
  $37.5M - $18.75M = $18.75M

Per Share Impact:
  $18.75M / 100M shares = $0.1875 per share
  Percentage: $0.1875 / $2.00 = 9.4% accretion
```

In this case, the deal is **accretive** — EPS improves by 9.4%.

### Variables That Drive Accretion/Dilution

| Variable | Effect | Example |
|----------|--------|---------|
| **Target Operating Margin** | Higher = More accretive | High-margin target more attractive |
| **Purchase Price Multiple** | Higher = More dilutive | Overpaying destroys accretion |
| **Buyer's Cost of Capital** | Higher = More dilutive | Expensive debt/equity financing kills deals |
| **Interest Rate** | Higher = More dilutive | Rising rates hurt leveraged deals |
| **Synergies** | Always accretive | Cost cuts, revenue gains improve math |
| **Tax Rate** | Higher = More dilutive | Tax expense reduces contribution |

## 2.5 M&A Synergies Analysis

### Synergies Definition and Importance

**Synergies**: Value created by combining two companies that didn't exist when operating separately. Synergies justify paying a premium above target's standalone value.

**Formula**:
```
Deal Price = Target Standalone Value + Synergies Value + Control Premium
```

**If Target Trading at $100M and Worth $120M Standalone:**
- Without synergies: Max price = $120M (buyer overpays otherwise)
- With $30M synergies: Justified price = $150M (both parties can share the $30M)

### Types of Synergies

#### 1. Revenue Synergies (Growth)

**Cross-Sell**: Buyer's customers buy target's products
```
Example: Bank acquires wealth manager
- Bank's 5,000 customers × 30% adoption × $50K average AUM = $75M new AUM
- At 50 bps fee = $375K annual run-rate revenue
```

**Pricing Power**: Elimination of competition allows price increases
```
Example: Two regional water utilities merge
- Combined entity = local monopoly
- Price increases 2-3% across 100,000 customers
- $100M revenue base × 2.5% = $2.5M annual benefit
```

**Market Expansion**: Buyer can use target's distribution to enter new markets
```
Example: US software company acquires European competitor
- Buyer's product now reaches 10M new users in Europe
- 5% penetration × $50 average revenue per user = $25M revenue
```

**Typical Revenue Synergy Realization**: 50-70% of projected (many are optimistic)

#### 2. Cost Synergies (Efficiency)

**Elimination of Duplicate Costs**: Post-merger, don't need duplicate functions
```
Example: Two retailers merge
- Eliminate one CFO, one HR VP, one IT director
- Savings: 3 × $300K salary = $900K
- Also: Eliminate one HQ office location = $5M annual savings
```

**Procurement Savings**: Larger combined entity gets better supplier pricing
```
Example: Combined COGS before 35% of revenue
- Supplier consolidation, volume discounts = 50 bps improvement
- Revenue $1B × 50 bps improvement = $5M savings
```

**Manufacturing Efficiency**: Consolidate production, leverage fixed costs
```
Example: Two manufacturers consolidate from 3 plants to 2
- Plant closure saves: $30M annual operating cost
- Redundant management: $5M
- Total: $35M cost reduction
```

**Typical Cost Synergy Realization**: 80-100% (more predictable than revenue)

#### 3. Working Capital Synergies

**Inventory Optimization**: Combined entity has lower working capital needs
```
Example: Retail merger
- Before: Two companies each hold 30 days inventory = 60 days combined
- After: Optimized to 35 days inventory
- 25-day reduction × $100M annual COGS / 365 = $6.8M cash release
```

**Receivables Management**: Stronger company can pay suppliers faster for discounts
```
Supplier offers: 2% discount if paid in 10 days vs. 30 days
Annual COGS: $500M
Discount value: $500M × 2% = $10M (requires $3.3M incremental working capital)
```

#### 4. Tax Synergies

**Loss Carryforwards**: Target has unused tax losses; buyer can use them
```
Target's tax losses: $50M
Tax rate: 25%
Tax benefit: $50M × 25% = $12.5M present value
```

**Asset Step-Up**: Fair value adjustment creates new tax basis; higher depreciation deductions
```
Purchase price allocation: Intangible asset $100M
Useful life: 15 years
Annual tax deduction: $6.7M
Tax shield (25% rate): $1.7M per year for 15 years
```

### Synergy Valuation & Deal Pricing

**NPV of Synergies**:
```
Synergies are typically:
1. Quantified year-by-year (Year 1: $5M, Year 2: $10M, Year 3: $15M)
2. Discounted at buyer's cost of capital (or higher for risk)
3. Summed to present value

Example:
Year 1 synergies: $5M / (1.10) = $4.5M
Year 2 synergies: $10M / (1.10)² = $8.3M
Year 3+ synergies: $15M perpetuity at 5% growth = $300M / 1.10² = $248M
Total NPV: ~$260M
```

**Synergy Discount Factors** (Reality Check):
- Revenue synergies: Apply 30-50% realization factor (risk of non-adoption)
- Cost synergies: Apply 80-90% realization factor (more controllable)
- Tax synergies: Apply 100% (mechanical, certain)

## 2.6 Valuation Methods for Transactions

### 1. Discounted Cash Flow (DCF)

**Approach**: Value = PV of all future free cash flows + Terminal Value

**Three Components**:

**a) Explicit Forecast Period (Years 1-5)**
```
Year 1 FCF: $50M / (1.08)¹ = $46.3M
Year 2 FCF: $55M / (1.08)² = $47.3M
Year 3 FCF: $60M / (1.08)³ = $47.6M
Year 4 FCF: $65M / (1.08)⁴ = $47.8M
Year 5 FCF: $70M / (1.08)⁵ = $47.6M
```

**b) Terminal Value** (Value of cash flows beyond Year 5)
```
Two methods:
1. Gordon Growth Model: TV = FCF(Year 5) × (1 + g) / (WACC - g)
   If Year 5 FCF = $70M, g = 2%, WACC = 8%:
   TV = $70M × 1.02 / (0.08 - 0.02) = $70M × 1.02 / 0.06 = $1,190M

2. Exit Multiple: TV = Year 5 EBITDA × Exit Multiple
   If Year 5 EBITDA = $100M, Exit Multiple = 10x:
   TV = $100M × 10 = $1,000M
```

**c) Present Value of Terminal Value**
```
PV(TV) = $1,190M / (1.08)⁵ = $810M
```

**Enterprise Value**:
```
EV = PV(Years 1-5 FCF) + PV(Terminal Value)
EV = ($46.3 + $47.3 + $47.6 + $47.8 + $47.6) + $810 = $237.6 + $810 = $1,047.6M

Equity Value = Enterprise Value - Net Debt
If Net Debt = $100M:
Equity Value = $1,047.6M - $100M = $947.6M
```

**DCF Sensitivity Analysis**: Small changes in WACC or terminal growth rate create large value swings
```
WACC = 7% vs. 9%: ~30% value difference
Terminal g = 1.5% vs. 2.5%: ~20% value difference
```

### 2. Comparable Company Analysis (Comps)

**Approach**: Value target based on multiples of similar public companies

**Step 1: Identify Comparable Public Companies**
- Same industry
- Similar size and growth profile
- Comparable business model

**Step 2: Extract Valuation Multiples**
```
EV/EBITDA: Enterprise Value / EBITDA
  Company A: $500M EV / $50M EBITDA = 10.0x
  Company B: $700M EV / $65M EBITDA = 10.8x
  Company C: $600M EV / $55M EBITDA = 10.9x
  Median: 10.8x
```

**Step 3: Apply Median Multiple to Target**
```
Target EBITDA: $50M
Valuation: $50M × 10.8x = $540M Enterprise Value
```

**Step 4: Derive Equity Value**
```
EV: $540M
Less: Net Debt: $75M
Equity Value: $465M
```

**Common Multiples Used**:
| Metric | Use Case |
|--------|----------|
| **EV/EBITDA** | All industries; operating performance |
| **EV/Revenue** | Startups, pre-EBITDA profitable |
| **EV/FCF** | Capital-intensive; cash generation focus |
| **P/E Ratio** | Mature, profitable companies |
| **EV/EBIT** | High-tax situations; operating leverage |

**Comps Median vs. Buyer-Specific Adjustment**:
```
Comps Median Valuation: $540M
Buyer Premium (15-30%): +$81M to +$162M (for synergies)
Justified Offer Price: $621M - $702M
```

### 3. Precedent Transactions

**Approach**: Value based on historical prices paid for similar acquisitions

**Step 1: Identify Historical Transactions**
- Similar targets in same or adjacent industry
- Similar transaction size
- Recent (past 3-5 years)

**Step 2: Calculate Transaction Multiples**
```
Purchase Price / EBITDA at time of transaction

Historical Deal: Company X acquired for $400M
Company X EBITDA at time: $50M
Implied Multiple: 8.0x

Historical Deal 2: Company Y acquired for $700M
Company Y EBITDA: $60M
Implied Multiple: 11.7x

Range: 8.0x - 11.7x
Median: ~10x
```

**Step 3: Apply to Target**
```
Target EBITDA: $50M
Valuation: $50M × 9.5x (mean of 8.0x and 11.7x) = $475M
```

**Premium Analysis**:
```
Precedent transactions typically show:
- Organic deals (strategic buyer): 20-35% premium over pre-announcement price
- Distressed/forced sales: 5-15% premium
- Competitive processes: 30-50% premiums
```

## 2.7 Three-Statement Financial Model Architecture

### Model Purpose

A financial model projects the target's financial statements forward under the sponsor's ownership and leverage structure. It answers:
1. How much cash does the business generate?
2. How much debt can we safely take on?
3. What returns can we achieve for equity?
4. What are the key drivers and sensitivities?

### Building the Model: 3-Statement Link

**Income Statement (P&L)** - Operating Performance
```
Revenue                                    $100M
- Cost of Goods Sold                       ($40M)  [40% margin]
Gross Profit                               $60M
- Operating Expenses (SG&A, etc.)          ($25M)
EBITDA                                     $35M
- Depreciation & Amortization              ($5M)
EBIT (Operating Income)                    $30M
- Interest Expense                         ($8M)   [debt financing cost]
- Other Expenses                           ($2M)
Pre-tax Income                             $20M
- Taxes (25%)                              ($5M)
Net Income                                 $15M
```

**Cash Flow Statement** - Cash Generation
```
Operating Cash Flow:
  Net Income                               $15M
  + D&A (non-cash add-back)                $5M
  - Change in Working Capital              ($2M)
Operating Cash Flow                        $18M

Investment Cash Flow:
  - Capital Expenditures (CapEx)           ($6M)
  Free Cash Flow                           $12M   [Available to pay debt]

Financing Cash Flow:
  - Principal Repayment                    ($8M)
  - Dividends to Sponsor                   ($2M)
  - Interest (already in P&L)              ($8M)
Net Cash Flow                              ($6M)

Cash Position:
  Beginning Cash                           $10M
  + Net Cash Flow                          ($6M)
Ending Cash                                $4M
```

**Balance Sheet** - Financial Position
```
ASSETS:
  Cash                                     $4M
  Accounts Receivable                      $15M
  Inventory                                $20M
  Other Current Assets                     $5M
  Fixed Assets (Net)                       $100M
  Goodwill & Intangibles                   $50M
Total Assets                               $194M

LIABILITIES & EQUITY:
  Accounts Payable                         $8M
  Accrued Expenses                         $5M
  Current Debt                             $10M
  Other Current Liabilities                $2M
  Long-term Debt                           $80M
  Other Liabilities                        $5M
  Equity                                   $84M
Total Liabilities & Equity                 $194M
```

### Key Relationships & Checks

**Working Capital Calculation**:
```
Change in Working Capital = (AR + Inventory - AP) / Revenue
If revenue grows 10%, working capital typically grows 10% (cash outflow)
```

**Debt Covenants & Calculations**:
```
Year 1: Total Debt = $90M, EBITDA = $35M → Leverage = 2.57x
Year 2: Total Debt = $82M (after $8M paydown), EBITDA = $38M → Leverage = 2.16x
Year 3: Total Debt = $74M, EBITDA = $42M → Leverage = 1.76x
[Leverage must stay <3.5x per debt agreement]
```

**Interest Expense**:
```
Average Debt Balance × Interest Rate = Annual Interest
Year 1: ($90M + $80M)/2 × 8% ≈ $6.8M
This interest flows to P&L as Interest Expense
```

# 3. FORMULAS & CALCULATIONS

## 3.1 LBO Analysis Formulas

### Internal Rate of Return (IRR)

**Definition**: The discount rate at which Net Present Value of equity cash flows = 0

**Formula (Implicit)**:
```
Initial Equity Investment = Σ[Distributions / (1+IRR)ⁿ] + [Exit Value / (1+IRR)ⁿ]
Where n = year of cash flow
```

**Interpretation**:
- 30% IRR = Equity investment grows 30% per year on a compounding basis
- 2.5x MOIC over 5 years ≈ 20% IRR
- 3.5x MOIC over 5 years ≈ 28% IRR

**In Practice**: Use Excel `IRR()` function on equity cash flows

**Example Calculation**:
```
Year 0: Investment = -$100M
Year 1-4: Interim distributions = $0
Year 5: Exit proceeds = $300M
IRR = Rate where: -$100M + $300M/(1+r)⁵ = 0
Solving: r ≈ 24.6%
```

### Multiple on Invested Capital (MOIC)

**Formula**:
```
MOIC = (Total Distributions + Exit Equity Proceeds) / Initial Equity Investment
```

**Calculation Example**:
```
Initial Equity: $100M
Year 1-4 Distributions: $10M per year = $40M total
Year 5 Exit Equity: $250M
Total Return: $40M + $250M = $290M
MOIC: $290M / $100M = 2.9x
```

**MOIC vs. IRR Conversion**:
```
MOIC = (1 + IRR)ⁿ (approximately, for hold period n)
2.9x MOIC over 5 years → ~24% IRR
3.0x MOIC over 5 years → ~25% IRR
```

### Debt Schedule Calculation

**Purpose**: Track debt balance year-by-year as it's repaid with free cash flow

**Template**:
```
Year          1         2         3         4         5
Beginning Debt    $500M    $450M    $395M    $330M    $255M
+ New Borrowing   $0       $0       $0       $0       $0
- Repayment       ($50M)   ($55M)   ($65M)   ($75M)   ($85M)  [≈80% of FCF]
Ending Debt       $450M    $395M    $330M    $255M    $170M
```

**Leverage Calculation**:
```
Year 1 Leverage = $450M Debt / $60M EBITDA = 7.5x (Initial leverage at acquisition)
Year 2 Leverage = $395M Debt / $65M EBITDA = 6.1x (Deleveraging via paydown)
Year 3 Leverage = $330M Debt / $70M EBITDA = 4.7x
Year 4 Leverage = $255M Debt / $75M EBITDA = 3.4x
Year 5 Leverage = $170M Debt / $80M EBITDA = 2.1x
```

### Free Cash Flow (FCF) Calculation

**Formula**:
```
Operating Cash Flow - Capital Expenditures - Changes in Working Capital = Free Cash Flow
```

**Expanded**:
```
Net Income                                    $50M
+ Depreciation & Amortization                $15M
- Changes in Working Capital                 ($5M)
= Operating Cash Flow                        $60M

- Capital Expenditures (CapEx)               ($10M)
= Free Cash Flow                             $50M
```

**Key**: FCF is available for debt service after maintaining the business

## 3.2 M&A Analysis Formulas

### Accretion/Dilution EPS Impact

**Formula**:
```
Target Net Income Contribution             = Target Operating Income × (1 - Tax Rate)
Financing Costs                             = Debt Issued × Interest Rate × (1 - Tax Rate)
Net Accretion/(Dilution)                    = Contribution - Financing Costs
EPS Impact per Share                        = Net Accretion/(Dilution) / Buyer Shares Outstanding
EPS % Accretion                             = EPS Impact / Current EPS
```

**Quick Test**:
```
Target P/E vs. Buyer Financing Rate:
- If Target P/E = 12x and Buyer Cost of Debt = 5%
- Buyer is getting 1/12 = 8.3% earnings yield on Target
- This exceeds 5% financing cost → Accretive
- If Buyer Cost of Debt = 10%, deal would be Dilutive
```

### Synergy Valuation NPV

**Formula**:
```
NPV(Synergies) = Σ[Synergies_year_n / (1 + Discount Rate)ⁿ] + Terminal Value
```

**Typical Discount Rate**: Buyer's WACC or risk-adjusted rate (higher for revenue synergies)

**Example**:
```
Year 1: $10M synergies / 1.08¹ = $9.3M
Year 2: $15M synergies / 1.08² = $12.9M
Year 3-5: $20M synergies / 1.08³ = $16.1M
Year 6+: $20M perpetuity at 3% growth = $20M × 1.03 / (0.08 - 0.03) = $412M / 1.08⁵ = $280M
Total NPV: $9.3 + $12.9 + $16.1 + $280 = $318.3M
```

### Deal Price Calculation

**Purchase Price Formula**:
```
Max Price = Target Standalone Value + (Synergies × Share with Target)
Typical "Share": 50/50 split of synergies between buyer and seller
```

**Example**:
```
Target Standalone Value: $500M
Synergies NPV: $200M
Fair Synergy Split: 50/50 = $100M to target, $100M to buyer
Max Price: $500M + $100M = $600M

Buyer's Downside: $600M - $100M value = $500M net cost
Buyer's Upside: $100M from synergies
Buyer's Return Requirement: >20% IRR
```

## 3.3 Valuation Multiple & Ratio Formulas

### Enterprise Value (EV)

**Formula**:
```
Enterprise Value = Market Cap + Total Debt - Cash & Cash Equivalents
```

**Alternatively** (for private companies):
```
EV = (Revenue or EBITDA or EBIT or FCF) × Multiple
```

**Typical Multiples by Industry**:
| Industry | EV/EBITDA | EV/Revenue |
|----------|-----------|-----------|
| Technology (High Growth) | 12-18x | 4-8x |
| Business Services | 10-14x | 2-4x |
| Manufacturing (Cyclical) | 6-10x | 0.8-1.5x |
| Utilities (Stable) | 10-12x | 2-3x |
| Retail | 5-8x | 0.3-0.8x |

### Debt/EBITDA Ratio

**Formula**:
```
Total Debt / EBITDA = Leverage Ratio
```

**Interpretation**:
- 4.0x: Requires 4 years of EBITDA to pay off debt (aggressive LBO)
- 3.0x: Requires 3 years of EBITDA (moderate LBO)
- 2.0x or Below: Conservative leverage

**In Context**:
```
Entry Leverage: 5.0x (highly leveraged LBO)
Year 1 Leverage: 4.5x (deleveraging via FCF)
Year 3 Leverage: 3.5x (further improvements)
Year 5 Target: 2.0x (sponsor can exit)
```

### Interest Coverage Ratio

**Formula**:
```
Interest Coverage = EBITDA / Annual Interest Expense
```

**Healthy Ranges**:
- >4.0x: Comfortable; easy debt service
- 2.5x - 4.0x: Moderate risk; standard LBO range
- <2.0x: High risk; vulnerable to downturn

**Calculation Example**:
```
EBITDA: $100M
Total Debt: $400M at 6% average rate
Annual Interest: $400M × 6% = $24M
Interest Coverage: $100M / $24M = 4.2x
Meaning: EBITDA covers interest payment 4.2 times
```

### Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR)

**Formula**:
```
DSCR = Free Cash Flow / (Interest + Principal Repayment)
```

**Interpretation**:
- >1.2x: Strong coverage; room for error
- 1.0x - 1.2x: Breakeven to tight coverage
- <1.0x: Negative cash flow; needs cash reserves or refinancing

**Example**:
```
FCF: $50M
Interest: $20M
Principal: $10M
DSCR: $50M / ($20M + $10M) = 1.67x
Meaning: FCF covers debt obligations 1.67 times
```

# 4. FRAMEWORKS & METHODOLOGIES

## 4.1 LBO Analysis Framework

**Step 1: Initial Screening**

| Criterion | Ideal Target |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Industry/Business Model** | Stable, recurring revenue (utilities, telecom) or predictable cash flows |
| **EBITDA Margins** | 25%+ (generates cash) vs. <10% (capital hungry) |
| **CapEx Intensity** | Low (5-8% of revenue) vs. High (>15%) |
| **Customer Concentration** | Diversified customer base (not 1-2 large customers) |
| **Market Position** | Market leader with pricing power; not distressed |
| **Growth Profile** | Stable (flat to 3%+) or attractive growth runway |

**Decision Rule**:
```
IF (Stable EBITDA > $50M
    AND EBITDA Margin > 25%
    AND CapEx < 10% Revenue
    AND No major customer concentration)
THEN → Candidate for LBO analysis
ELSE → Likely too cash-hungry or risky
```

**Step 2: Build 5-Year Financial Projection**

| Input | Assumption | Driver |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Revenue Growth | 2-5% annually | Industry growth + market share |
| EBITDA Margin | Hold flat or improve 50 bps/year | Operational improvements |
| CapEx | 6-8% of revenue | Maintenance level |
| Working Capital | 5-8% of revenue change | Growth requirement |
| Tax Rate | 25-27% | Jurisdictional |

**Step 3: Determine Debt Capacity**

**Approach**:
```
Maximum Debt = Minimum(
  6.0x Entry Leverage (based on entry EBITDA),
  Maintains 3.0x Leverage at exit,
  Maintains 2.5x Interest Coverage throughout,
  DSCR > 1.1x every year
)
```

**Example**:
```
Target Entry EBITDA: $60M
6.0x Entry Leverage = $360M maximum debt
If company is projected to grow 3% and delever through FCF:
Exit Leverage (Year 5): $360M Debt / $70M EBITDA = 5.1x (too high)
Reduce debt to $210M:
Entry Leverage: $210M / $60M = 3.5x
Exit Leverage: $210M / $70M = 3.0x ✓
```

**Step 4: Calculate Equity Returns**

**Sources of Return**:
1. Debt Paydown: $210M → $120M (principal reduction captures debt value)
2. EBITDA Growth: $60M → $70M (operational improvement)
3. Multiple Expansion: 10x entry → 11x exit (market favorable)

**Exit Enterprise Value**:
```
Year 5 EBITDA: $70M
Exit Multiple: 11.0x
Exit EV: $770M
```

**Exit Equity Value**:
```
Exit EV: $770M
Less: Remaining Debt: $120M
Exit Equity: $650M
```

**Equity Return Calculation**:
```
Entry Equity: $200M (financed with $210M debt)
Exit Equity: $650M
MOIC: $650M / $200M = 3.25x
IRR over 5 years: ~26%
```

**Step 5: Sensitivity Analysis**

Test returns under various scenarios:

**Base Case**: As calculated above (3.25x MOIC, 26% IRR)

**Downside Case** (Lower multiples, slower deleveraging):
```
Exit Multiple: 9.0x vs. 11.0x
Exit EV: $70M × 9.0x = $630M
Exit Equity: $630M - $200M debt = $430M
MOIC: 2.15x IRR: ~17%
```

**Upside Case** (Higher growth, multiple expansion):
```
Year 5 EBITDA: $75M (stronger growth)
Exit Multiple: 12.0x
Exit EV: $900M
Exit Equity: $900M - $100M debt = $800M
MOIC: 4.0x IRR: ~33%
```

## 4.3 Financial Modeling Best Practices

### Model Structure (Architecture)

**Recommended Tabs**:
1. **Inputs**: All assumptions (growth rates, margins, leverage targets)
2. **P&L**: Income statement projection
3. **CF Statement**: Cash flow projection and debt paydown schedule
4. **Balance Sheet**: Assets, liabilities, equity account
5. **Debt Schedule**: Detailed debt balances, interest calculations
6. **Output/Summary**: IRR, MOIC, leverage path, returns
7. **Sensitivity Tables**: IRR/MOIC under various scenarios
8. **Sources & Uses**: Initial financing at acquisition

### Formula Design (Best Practices)

**1. Always Link Statements**:
```
P&L → CF (Net Income becomes input to Operating Cash Flow)
CF → Balance Sheet (FCF determines Cash position, Debt paydown)
Balance Sheet → P&L (Interest expense = Debt × Rate)

Avoid: Hard-coding numbers; use formulas to ensure integrity
```

**2. Use Consistent Assumptions Tab**:
```
[Inputs]
Revenue Growth: 3%
EBITDA Margin: 32%
CapEx % of Revenue: 7%
Tax Rate: 25%

[Model]
Year 1 Revenue = Prior Year Revenue × (1 + Revenue Growth)
Uses same input everywhere → One change updates entire model
```

**3. Clear Debt Schedule**:
```
Beginning Debt Balance
+ New Borrowings (Refinancings)
- Principal Repayments (FCF based)
= Ending Debt Balance

Interest = Ending Debt Balance × Interest Rate
Leverage = Debt Balance / EBITDA
```

**4. Avoid Circular References**:
```
Don't: Make Interest Expense depend on FCF depend on Interest (loops)
Do: Calculate Interest based on prior-year debt balance
    Then use Interest to calculate current-year FCF
```

### Common Modeling Errors to Avoid

| Error | Impact | Fix |
|-------|--------|-----|
| **Hard-coded numbers** | Changes don't cascade | Use formulas + inputs tab |
| **Wrong CapEx assumption** | FCF overstated/understated | Match historical % of revenue |
| **Ignoring working capital** | Cash flow inaccurate | Model as % of revenue change |
| **Interest on ending debt** | Next period interest wrong | Use average or lagged debt |
| **Tax rate inconsistency** | Net income doesn't flow | Use single tax rate consistently |
| **No debt schedule** | Can't track leverage covenant | Explicit year-by-year schedule |

# 5. KEY RELATIONSHIPS & INSIGHTS

## 5.1 Leverage and Returns: The Trade-off

### Higher Leverage = Higher Returns (and Higher Risk)

**The Math**:
```
Equity Return = (Operating Return × Asset Value - Debt Cost × Debt) / Equity Value
             = (ROIC × Assets - r_d × Debt) / Equity
```

**Example**:
```
Company ROIC: 15% (return on assets)
Debt Cost: 6%

Scenario 1: All-Equity (0% debt)
Equity Value = $500M, Debt = $0
Equity Return = 15% × $500M / $500M = 15%

Scenario 2: 50% Debt Financing
Debt = $250M, Equity = $250M
Equity Return = (15% × $500M - 6% × $250M) / $250M = ($75M - $15M) / $250M = 24%

Scenario 3: 75% Debt Financing
Debt = $375M, Equity = $125M
Equity Return = (15% × $500M - 6% × $375M) / $125M = ($75M - $22.5M) / $125M = 42%
```

**Key Insight**: If ROIC (15%) > Cost of Debt (6%), leverage increases equity returns.

**The Risk**:
```
If ROIC falls to 8% (below debt cost 6%), leverage is bad:
Scenario 3: Equity Return = (8% × $500M - 6% × $375M) / $125M = ($40M - $22.5M) / $125M = 14%
Equity fully wiped out if ROIC < Debt Cost at high leverage
```

**LBO Implication**: High leverage (4-6x) works great in stable/growing businesses; dangerous if ROIC declines.

## 5.2 Accretion/Dilution Waterfall

**How Synergies Turn Dilutive Deals into Accretive**:

```
Baseline (No Deal):
  Buyer EPS: $2.00

Year 1 Without Synergies (Dilutive):
  Target contributes $1.50 per share (its EPS)
  Financing cost: ($0.60) per share
  Net Year 1 EPS: $2.00 + $1.50 - $0.60 = $2.90 (+45% accretive!)

Wait, that's accretive. Let me recalculate with Dilutive Scenario:

Dilutive Scenario (Overpaid Target):
  Target EPS: $1.00 per share (weak company)
  Financing cost: ($1.20) per share
  Year 1 EPS Impact: +$1.00 - $1.20 = -$0.20 (Dilutive)
  Buyer EPS: $2.00 - $0.20 = $1.80

Year 3 With Synergies Realized:
  Target Base EPS: $1.00
  + Synergies: $0.50 per share (cost cuts, cross-sell)
  = Target with Synergies: $1.50
  - Financing Cost: $1.20
  Net: +$0.30 per share
  Buyer EPS: $2.00 + $0.30 = $2.30 (Accretive)
```

**Framework**:
| Year | Target Alone | Financing Cost | Synergies | Net EPS Impact |
|------|--------------|----------------|-----------|----------------|
| **Year 1** | $1.00 | ($1.20) | $0.00 | -$0.20 |
| **Year 2** | $1.00 | ($1.15) | $0.25 | +$0.10 |
| **Year 3** | $1.00 | ($1.10) | $0.50 | +$0.40 |

**Conclusion**: Dilutive deals become accretive when synergies materialize.

## 5.3 Valuation Method Hierarchy (Which One Is Right?)

### When to Use Each Method

**DCF** (Best for strategic analysis):
- Inputs: explicit forecast + terminal growth
- Strength: Captures long-term value
- Weakness: Sensitive to terminal value (60-70% of EV); small growth rate changes = big value swings
- Use When: Planning 5+ year hold; confident in growth assumptions

**Comparable Companies** (Best for benchmark):
- Inputs: Current market multiples
- Strength: Market-tested, widely accepted
- Weakness: Public company prices may not apply to target (different growth, profitability)
- Use When: Want "market consensus" valuation; quick check

**Precedent Transactions** (Best for recent market):
- Inputs: Historical deal prices
- Strength: Real transaction prices (includes buyer premiums/synergies)
- Weakness: Limited comparables in any industry; historical data (market changes)
- Use When: Multiple similar deals closed recently in same sector

### Reconciliation Approach

**Typical Framework**:
```
Valuation Range = [
  Comps Low (7.5x EBITDA) to Comps High (12.0x EBITDA),
  Precedent Low (8.0x) to Precedent High (11.0x),
  DCF Valuation ± 20%
]

Example:
  Comps: $375M - $600M (median $487M)
  Precedents: $400M - $550M (median $475M)
  DCF: $550M ± $110M = $440M - $660M
  Synthesis: $450M - $600M fair value range

Buyer Bid: $500M (mid-range, reasonable)
```

## 5.4 Sources of LBO Value Creation: The Sponsor's Perspective

**Four Buckets of Return**:

1. **Financial Engineering (Leverage)**:
   - Buying at 8x EBITDA with 60% debt financing (4.8x debt/EBITDA)
   - Even with no growth, debt paydown and disciplined capital allocation improve returns
   - Typical contribution: 20-30% of total return in stable company

2. **Operational Improvements**:
   - Cost reductions (supply chain, overheads, overhead consolidation)
   - Revenue growth (market share gains, new products, pricing improvements)
   - Working capital optimization (inventory, receivables)
   - Typical contribution: 30-50% of total return in turnaround

3. **Exit Multiple Expansion**:
   - Buyer at 8x, exits at 10x (market favorable, company de-risked, growth trajectory proven)
   - Benefit = Exit multiple - Entry multiple
   - Typical contribution: 20-30% in favorable markets; zero in deteriorating markets

4. **Dividend Recaps** (Optional):
   - After deleveraging Year 1-2, refinance at higher leverage to distribute cash
   - Sponsor extracts equity value while maintaining deal IRR
   - Typical contribution: 0-20% (only in over-levered LBOs)

**Which Matters Most?**
```
Stable business: Financial engineering (60%) + OpEx (30%) + Multiple (10%)
Turnaround: OpEx improvements (60%) + Financial engineering (25%) + Multiple (15%)
Growth story: Growth (50%) + Financial engineering (30%) + Multiple (20%)
```

```

```

```
  Year 1-5 Revenue CAGR: 8%

  CapEx: 4% of revenue (asset-light model)
  Working Capital: 10% of revenue change

  Year 1 Revenue: $108M, EBITDA: $27M, FCF: $18M

  Year 3 Revenue: $126M, EBITDA: $31.5M, FCF: $21M
  Year 4 Revenue: $136M, EBITDA: $34M, FCF: $23M
  Year 5 Revenue: $147M, EBITDA: $36.75M, FCF: $24.5M

Terminal Value (Gordon Growth):
  Year 5 FCF × (1 + 2.5% growth) / (7.5% - 2.5%) = $24.5M × 1.025 / 5% = $501.75M
  PV of TV: $501.75M / 1.075⁵ = $363M

  PV of FCF Year 1-5: $18/1.075 + $20/1.075² + $21/1.075³ + $23/1.075⁴ + $24.5/1.075⁵
                    = $16.7M + $17.3M + $17.8M + $17.8M + $17.2M = $86.8M
  PV of Terminal Value: $363M

```

# 8. GLOSSARY OF KEY TERMS

## Financial Metrics
- **EBITDA**: Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, Amortization; operating cash profit
- **EBIT**: Operating income = EBITDA - D&A
- **FCF**: Free Cash Flow = Operating CF - CapEx - ∆ Working Capital; cash available for debt service
- **Net Debt**: Total Debt - Cash & Cash Equivalents
- **Enterprise Value (EV)**: Total firm value = Market Cap + Net Debt
- **Leverage Ratio**: Total Debt / EBITDA; measures debt relative to operating profit
- **Interest Coverage**: EBITDA / Interest Expense; measures debt service ability

## LBO-Specific Terms
- **Sponsor**: Private equity firm or buyer funding the LBO
- **Entry Leverage**: Debt/EBITDA ratio at acquisition
- **MOIC**: Multiple on Invested Capital = Exit Value / Entry Investment
- **IRR**: Internal Rate of Return; annualized equity return
- **Debt Service**: Annual interest + principal repayment
- **Debt Paydown**: Reduction of debt balance through operating cash flow
- **Sources & Uses**: Statement matching financing sources to uses of funds
- **Equity Contribution**: Sponsor's cash investment at entry

## M&A-Specific Terms
- **Synergies**: Value created by combining two companies
- **Accretion/Dilution**: Impact of acquisition on buyer's EPS
- **Purchase Price**: Total consideration paid for target
- **Earn-out**: Contingent payment based on post-acquisition performance
- **Walk-away Price**: Maximum price buyer will pay before deal becomes uneconomical
- **Collar**: Price range with buyer downside protection

## Valuation Terms
- **EV/EBITDA Multiple**: Most common valuation metric in M&A
- **DCF**: Discounted Cash Flow; values company based on NPV of future cash flows
- **Terminal Value**: Value of cash flows beyond explicit forecast period
- **WACC**: Weighted Average Cost of Capital; discount rate for DCF
- **Comparable Companies**: Public company multiples used to value private companies
- **Precedent Transactions**: Historical M&A prices used as valuation benchmark

## Debt-Related Terms
- **Senior Debt**: First-lien secured debt; lowest cost, highest priority
- **Mezzanine Debt**: Subordinated debt; higher yield, equity-like features
- **Covenant**: Contractual restriction (max leverage, min interest coverage, etc.)
- **Refinancing**: Replacing existing debt with new financing at better terms
- **Amortization**: Mandatory debt paydown schedule
- **Revolver/Credit Facility**: Flexible borrowing arrangement; draw as needed

## Deal Structure Terms
- **Stock Purchase**: Buyer acquires all stock; assumes all liabilities
- **Asset Purchase**: Buyer acquires specific assets; seller retains liabilities
- **Merger**: Target absorbed into buyer; ceases to exist
- **Leveraged Buyout (LBO)**: Acquisition financed primarily with debt
- **Management Buyout (MBO)**: LBO by existing management team
- **Going-Private**: Public company acquired by buyer; delisted
