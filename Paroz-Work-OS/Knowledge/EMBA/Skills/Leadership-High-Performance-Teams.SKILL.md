## Core Framework: What Makes High-Performing Teams

### Five Dysfunctions of a Team (Lencioni)

Required pre-reading. The five dysfunctions form a pyramid:

```
               5. INATTENTION TO RESULTS
              (Status & ego over team goals)
           4. AVOIDANCE OF ACCOUNTABILITY
          (Low standards, no peer pressure)
        3. LACK OF COMMITMENT
       (Ambiguity, no buy-in to decisions)
     2. FEAR OF CONFLICT
    (Artificial harmony, no productive debate)
  1. ABSENCE OF TRUST
 (Invulnerability, hiding weaknesses)
```

**Key insight:** Trust is the foundation. Without vulnerability-based trust, teams cannot engage in productive conflict, which means they can't achieve genuine commitment, which means they won't hold each other accountable, which means they won't focus on collective results.

## The Team Performance Model

### Three Measures of Team Performance

| Measure | Definition |
|---------|-----------|
| **Individual Accountability** | Each member meets commitments, delivers quality work, meets deadlines |
| **Team Alignment** | Shared understanding of goals, roles, norms, and expectations |
| **Team Engagement** | Active participation, psychological safety, constructive debate |

### Effective and Supportive Team Member Behaviors

The course defines specific behavioral expectations:

**Communicate:**
- Discuss/debate to solve problems, not to be right
- Treat conflict as learning opportunity
- Fulfill team roles (Lead, Second, Chair, Scribe, Contributor) effectively
- Uphold team norms that contribute to performance

**Adapt:**
- Hold others accountable when work doesn't meet standards
- Watch for opportunities to help teammates
- Contribute to debriefing and lessons learned
- Communicate openly and share information timely

**Relate:**
- Treat all members with respect and as equals
- Meet deadlines reliably
- Deliver thorough and accurate work
- Manage personal conflict productively

**Educate:**
- Provide constructive feedback (timely and specific)
- Provide positive feedback and encouragement
- Ask for feedback and use it to change behavior
- Contribute knowledge and seek ideas from all members

## Social Contracts for Teams

### Why Social Contracts Work (HBR)

A **social contract** is an explicit agreement that lays out ground rules for team members' behaviors — how members will work together, make decisions, communicate, share information, and support each other.

**Benefits:**
- Give employees a sense of control and security
- Instill responsibility, accountability, and trust
- Motivate desirable behaviors without heavy-handed surveillance
- Reduce negative behaviors (gossip, undermining, disengagement)

**Key principles:**
- Cannot be mandated by the leader — must be collectively formed
- Must include procedures for handling violations
- Must be regularly updated to improve effectiveness
- Leader must model the desired behaviors

### Team Contract Components

Teams develop contracts covering:
1. **Shared values and mission** — What does this team stand for?
2. **Communication norms** — Response times, channels, meeting formats
3. **Decision-making process** — Consensus, majority, leader decides?
4. **Role expectations** — Lead, Second, Chair, Scribe rotations
5. **Conflict resolution** — How to address disagreements
6. **Accountability mechanisms** — How to address missed commitments
7. **Quality standards** — Expectations for deliverable quality

## DiSC Personality Assessment

### The Four DiSC Styles

| Style | Priorities | Motivated By | Stressed By |
|-------|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| **D** (Dominance) | Results, action, challenge | Control, competition, achievement | Loss of control, vulnerability |
| **i** (Influence) | Enthusiasm, collaboration, action | Recognition, social approval, excitement | Being ignored, disapproval |
| **S** (Steadiness) | Support, stability, collaboration | Harmony, helping others, security | Rapid change, conflict |
| **C** (Conscientiousness) | Accuracy, stability, challenge | Quality, expertise, correctness | Being wrong, criticism |

### DiSC in Conflict — Destructive Behaviors

The "Under the Hood" e-book reveals the **hidden psychological mechanisms** driving destructive conflict responses:

**Common destructive behaviors and their hidden rewards:**
| Behavior | Hidden Reward |
|----------|-------------|
| **Gossiping** | Catharsis, validation from allies, perceived power/authority |
| **Sarcasm** | Take shots without commitment; "just kidding" immunity |
| **Stonewalling** | Punish the other person; avoid messy emotional engagement; self-preservation |
| **Exaggerating** | Make the situation sound as bad as it feels; strengthen your case |

**Two hidden mechanisms:**
1. **Emotional Indulgence** — We stay angry because it feels rewarding (self-righteousness activates pleasure centers in the brain alongside disgust)
2. **Emotional Avoidance** — We choose anger/dismissal to avoid deeper feelings of hurt or inadequacy that threaten our sense of self-worth

### Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) for Conflict

The solution framework from the DiSC conflict model:

```
Step 1: RECOGNIZE unhealthy automatic thoughts
        (Strong emotions = signal to slow down and reflect)
        Ask: Is this thought valid/true? Is there another way to see this?

Step 2: CATCH AND REFRAME the automatic thought
        Replace with more realistic, productive thought
        Example: "They have no idea what they're talking about"
              → "They're coming at this from a different angle than me"

Step 3: CHOOSE a more productive response
        Emotional reaction realigns to actual (not perceived) threat
        From lashing out → assertively stating needs
        From retreating → constructively expressing concerns
```

## Self-Improvement Framework for Teams (HBR)

### Three Foundational Capabilities

From "To Improve Your Team, First Work on Yourself":

| Capability | Description | Questions to Ask |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Internal Self-Awareness** | Understanding your own feelings, beliefs, values, and inner narrative | What emotions am I experiencing? What am I assuming? What are facts vs. interpretations? What are my core values? |
| **External Self-Awareness** | Understanding how your words and actions impact others | What am I doing that is helpful? Not helpful? What would you change about how I interact? |
| **Personal Accountability** | Holding yourself (not just others) responsible | Am I part of the problem? What's my contribution to this situation? What can I do differently? |

**Action framework when frustrated:**
1. Explore reactions → internal self-awareness
2. Consider impact on others → external self-awareness
3. Assess own contribution → personal accountability → choose conscious response

## Shared Values and High Performance (HBR)

### Key Findings

- **Teams, not individuals, get results** — especially in creative, complex, and analytical work
- **Diverse teams outperform homogeneous ones** — but only when managed well
- The **diversity paradox**: diverse teams are more innovative BUT face more conflict and resistance
- Resolution: Build a **culture of shared values** — hire people who share your mission, not just skills
- Values alignment provides cohesion that enables productive diversity

### The McKinsey "War for Talent" Lesson

Focusing on individual "A players" over team performance created perverse incentives at Enron. Lesson: Team culture > individual brilliance.

## Team Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|------|---------------|
| **Lead** | Primary responsibility for assignment quality and direction; coordinates the team's effort |
| **Second** | Supports the Lead; serves as quality check and backup; ensures nothing falls through cracks |
| **Chair** | Runs team meetings; sets agenda; manages time; ensures all voices are heard |
| **Scribe** | Documents decisions, action items, and key discussion points |
| **Contributor** | Active participation; delivers individual portions on time and to standard |

Roles rotate across assignments to develop all members' skills.

## 360 Peer Evaluation Framework

The fall 360 feedback evaluates team members across 25 behavioral items in four categories:

| Category | Sample Items |
|----------|-------------|
| **Communicate** | Debates to solve (not to be right), treats conflict as learning, fulfills team roles, upholds norms |
| **Adapt** | Holds others accountable, offers help proactively, contributes to debriefs, communicates openly |
| **Relate** | Treats members as equals, meets deadlines, delivers quality work, manages conflict productively |
| **Educate** | Provides timely constructive feedback, asks for feedback, acquires new skills, seeks consensus |

**Rating scale:** 1 (rarely exhibits) to 5 (consistently exceptional, inspires teammates)

Plus two open-ended questions:
1. What change in behavior would have the most positive impact?
2. What is the most impactful positive behavior they bring to the team?
