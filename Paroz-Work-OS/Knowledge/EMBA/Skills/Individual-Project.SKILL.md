## Course Architecture

**Capstone individual project** where students apply all MBA concepts to a real business problem. Self-directed course with an assigned Project Advisor.

**Two project options:**

| Option | Full Name | Description |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| **MCP** | Management Consulting Project | Solve a real business problem within a defined organization |
| **NVP** | New Venture Project | Develop a real business opportunity (new business, new venture within existing org, or new product) |

**MCP Criteria:**
- Real, substantive business problem within a defined organization
- Sufficient scope to demonstrate MBA-level analysis
- Demonstrates understanding of organizational context and strategy

**NVP Criteria:**
- Real, substantive business opportunity (new business, new venture within existing organization, or new product)
- Sufficient scope to demonstrate MBA-level analysis

**Key distinction:** Individual effort only (rare two-person exception requires prior approval).

## Project Process

The project follows a structured four-step process with defined milestones:

### Step 1: Proposal (November 25, 2024)

**Requirements:**
- Maximum 2 pages
- Not graded but mandatory

**Content to address:**
- Who is the client?
- What is the problem or opportunity?
- Why are you interested in this project?
- What value will you add?
- Can you conduct both primary and secondary research?
- Which MBA concepts will you apply?

### Step 2: Work Plan (January 31, 2025)

**Requirements:**
- 8-10 pages
- Worth 5% of final grade (assessed as project management score)

**Content to include:**
- Objectives with timelines
- Preliminary framework or table of contents
- Description of added value
- List of MBA concepts to be applied
- Explanation of project scope

### Step 3: Consulting Report / Business Plan (June 2, 2025)

**Requirements:**
- Worth 75% of final grade
- Must be well-written prose, not point-form

**Mandatory components:**
1. **Transmittal Letter** — Formal cover letter to the client
2. **Executive Summary** — Standalone summary of key findings and recommendations
3. **Table of Contents** — Complete document navigation
4. **Body** — Full analysis with feasible recommendations
5. **Appendices** — Supporting data, analytical tools, supplementary materials

### Step 4: Pitch Presentation (July 14, 2025)

**Requirements:**
- Worth 20% of final grade
- 20 minutes presentation + 10 minutes Q&A

**Format:**
- Present to a newly assigned Pitch Evaluator (different from Project Advisor)
- Evaluator plays a specific role: banker for NVP, department head for MCP
- Objective is to sell the idea and recommendations

## Deliverable Requirements

### Report Quality Standards

- **Prose over bullets:** The report must be written as well-structured prose, not point-form notes
- **Analytical tools placement:** SWOT analysis, PEST analysis, and similar frameworks should NOT appear directly in the body; apply findings in context within the body and place the raw analysis in appendices
- **Feasible recommendations:** Emphasis on recommendations that the client can realistically implement
- **Primary + secondary research:** Both types of research are expected

### Grading Scale

| Grade | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **B** | Fully satisfactory project meeting all requirements |
| **A** | Exceeds expectations in quality, depth, or originality |

### Rules and Policies

- **Individual effort:** Projects are completed individually (rare two-person exception with approval)
- **ChatGPT usage:** Must be cited; heavy reliance on generative AI risks failure
- **Late penalty:** 2% per day
- **Completeness:** All four steps must be completed

---

## Key Rules

1. **SWOT/PEST in appendices, not body** — The raw analytical framework output goes in appendices; the body should integrate findings into contextual analysis and narrative
2. **Cite all AI usage** — Any use of ChatGPT or similar tools must be explicitly cited; over-reliance risks a failing grade
3. **Sell, don't just report** — The pitch presentation requires persuading the evaluator in their assigned role, not merely presenting findings
4. **Transmittal letter is mandatory** — Professional letter addressed to the client, separate from the executive summary
5. **Executive summary stands alone** — Must be comprehensible without reading the full report
6. **Feasibility is paramount** — Recommendations that the client cannot realistically implement will weaken the project

## Paroz's Project

### Project Type and Title

**Management Consulting Project:** "The Regulated Innovator's Playbook: Transforming Financial Services Through Lakehouse-Powered Data Democratization"

### Client

**Questrade** — Canadian financial services firm

### Problem Statement

600+ Active Directory groups creating access management complexity, blocking data democratization across the organization.

### Proposed Solution

Transform from the existing fragmented access model to a four-role RBAC (Role-Based Access Control) model, enabling broader data access while maintaining regulatory compliance.

### Quantified Value

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Annual savings | $1.2-1.5M |
| IRR | 42% |
| Payback period | 12 months |
| Admin cost reduction | 70% |
| Data user expansion | 28% to 70% of eligible workforce |
| Decision speed improvement | 3.5x faster |
| Success probability (Monte Carlo) | 97% |

## MBA Concept Integration

Paroz's project integrates concepts from across the EMBA curriculum. This is the defining characteristic of the capstone — demonstrating breadth and depth of MBA learning applied to a real problem.

### Strategy META Framework

| Framework | Source Course | Application in Project |
|-----------|-------------|----------------------|
| **PEST Analysis** | Business Strategy | Macro-environmental scan of Canadian financial services (regulatory, technology, economic, social factors affecting data governance) |
| **Five Forces** | Business Strategy | Competitive dynamics in financial data platform market; vendor power analysis (Temenos, Quantiphi, Databricks) |
| **VRIN Analysis** | Business Strategy | Assessment of Questrade's data capabilities as strategic resources; evaluating whether the lakehouse architecture creates sustainable competitive advantage |

### Organizational Analysis

| Framework | Source Course | Application in Project |
|-----------|-------------|----------------------|
| **Congruence Model** | Managing & Leading Orgs | Diagnosing the misalignment between Questrade's data strategy (formal organization) and its 600+ AD group access structure (actual work processes); identifying root causes of data democratization barriers |

### Problem-Solving and Analysis

| Framework | Source Course | Application in Project |
|-----------|-------------|----------------------|
| **IDEALS Framework** | Critical Thinking | Structured problem identification, root cause determination, option enumeration, and recommendation assessment for the RBAC transformation |

### Technology and Systems

| Framework | Source Course | Application in Project |
|-----------|-------------|----------------------|
| **Enterprise Architecture** | MIS | Lakehouse architecture design; Medallion schema (Bronze/Silver/Gold); platform-as-a-service model for data access |
| **Socio-Technical Systems** | MIS | Balancing technical RBAC implementation with organizational change management; understanding that the 600+ AD groups are a socio-technical problem, not purely technical |

### Financial Analysis

| **Monte Carlo Simulation** | Business Decision Models | 97% success probability estimate through simulation of implementation scenarios |

### Business Planning and Communication

| Framework | Source Course | Application in Project |
|-----------|-------------|----------------------|
