## The Full-Range Leadership Model

The course builds on the **Full-Range Leadership Model**, which arranges leadership behaviors from least to most effective:

```
LEAST EFFECTIVE                                              MOST EFFECTIVE
├── Passive/Avoidant ──┤── Transactional ──┤── Transformational ──┤
│                      │                   │                      │
│ Laissez-faire        │ Contingent Reward │ Idealized Influence  │
│                      │ MBE-Active        │ Inspirational Motiv. │
│                      │ MBE-Passive       │ Intellectual Stim.   │
│                      │                   │ Indiv. Consideration │
```

## Module 1: Transactional Leadership

### Laissez-Faire Leadership (Passive Leadership)

The **absence** of leadership — avoidance of decision-making, withdrawal from responsibility.

**Consequences of laissez-faire leadership:**
- Team confusion and lack of direction
- Lower morale and engagement
- Increased workplace conflict (vacuum of authority)
- Reduced productivity and performance

**Key insight:** Laissez-faire is NOT the same as delegation. Delegation involves intentional empowerment with clear expectations; laissez-faire is disengagement.

### Contingent Reward

Leadership based on **exchange** — leader provides rewards (praise, recognition, compensation) in return for agreed-upon performance.

**Key elements:**
- Set clear expectations using **SMART goals** (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound)
- Provide feedback (not just advice)
- Recognize and reward achievement
- **Feedback vs. Advice distinction:** Feedback is information about past performance; advice is guidance about future behavior. Both serve different purposes.

### Management-by-Exception (MBE)

Two forms:
- **MBE-Active:** Leader monitors performance and intervenes when standards are not met. Proactive error detection.
- **MBE-Passive:** Leader intervenes only when problems become serious or are brought to attention. Reactive.

**TIPP Skills for MBE:**
- **T**imely intervention
- **I**mmediate response
- **P**roportional correction
- **P**rivate discussion (not public correction)

## Module 2: Transformational Leadership — The 4 I's

Transformational leadership **inspires followers to transcend self-interest for the good of the organization** and achieves performance beyond expectations.

### 1. Idealized Influence (Ethical Leadership)

Leader serves as a **role model** through ethical behavior, high standards, and personal sacrifice.

- Followers identify with and want to emulate the leader
- Based on trust, admiration, and respect
- Leader demonstrates conviction and takes stands on important issues
- Walks the talk — alignment between words and actions

### 2. Inspirational Motivation (Inspirational Leadership)

Leader **articulates a compelling vision** that energizes and motivates followers.

- Communicates high expectations
- Uses symbols and emotional appeals to focus effort
- Creates meaning and challenge in followers' work
- Expresses confidence that goals will be achieved
- Team spirit is aroused through enthusiasm and optimism

### 3. Intellectual Stimulation (Developmental Leadership)

Leader **challenges assumptions** and encourages creativity and innovation.

- Encourages followers to question established approaches
- Reframes problems to find new perspectives
- Solicits new ideas without criticizing individual contributions
- Fosters independent thinking and problem-solving
- Does NOT publicly criticize mistakes

### 4. Individualized Consideration (Relational Leadership)

Leader acts as **coach and mentor**, attending to each follower's needs for growth and achievement.

- Recognizes individual differences in needs and desires
- Creates new learning opportunities
- Practices active listening
- Delegates tasks as growth opportunities
- Provides personalized attention to each team member

## Module 3: Charismatic Leadership

### The Romance of Leadership

Before teaching charismatic behaviors, the course addresses an important caveat: **the romance of leadership** — the tendency to over-attribute organizational outcomes (positive and negative) to leaders while underestimating situational and systemic factors.

### Verbal Charismatic Leadership Tactics (CLTs)

Research-validated verbal behaviors that enhance perceived charisma:

1. **Metaphors and analogies** — Make abstract ideas concrete and relatable
2. **Stories and anecdotes** — Create emotional connection through narrative
3. **Moral conviction** — Express beliefs about right and wrong
4. **Reflections of group sentiments** — Articulate what the audience is feeling
5. **Setting high expectations** — Communicate ambitious goals with confidence
6. **Communicating confidence** — Express certainty that goals can be achieved
7. **Three-part lists** — Group ideas in threes for memorability and rhythm
8. **Rhetorical questions** — Engage audience thinking without expecting answers
9. **Contrasts** — Juxtapose ideas to create clarity and emphasis

### Non-Verbal Charismatic Leadership Tactics

1. **Animated voice** — Vary pitch, pace, volume, and pauses
2. **Facial expressions** — Use expressions that match the emotional content
3. **Gestures** — Use purposeful hand and body movements to emphasize points
4. **Body posture** — Open, confident stance; lean toward audience
5. **Eye contact** — Direct, sustained eye contact with individuals

### Charismatic Leadership Speech Assignment

**Format:** 3-minute speech introducing a new project idea to your EMBA Learning Team (as if they were your actual work team) and asking for their support.

**Evaluation criteria:**
- Verbal charismatic behaviors (CLTs used effectively)
- Non-verbal delivery (voice, gestures, eye contact, posture)
- Persuasiveness and audience engagement
- Time management (2% penalty per 10 seconds over 3 minutes)

**Preparation time:** 1.5 hours to prepare and rehearse

## Key Concepts & Distinctions

### Abusive Supervision / Pseudo-Transformational Leadership

The course also covers the **dark side** of leadership:
- **Abusive supervision:** Sustained hostile verbal/non-verbal behaviors (excluding physical contact)
- **Pseudo-transformational leadership:** Leaders who appear transformational but serve their own interests rather than followers' — narcissistic, manipulative, exploitative

### The Norm of Reciprocity in Leadership

People tend to **give back what they receive**. Positive leadership behaviors (recognition, trust, development) create reciprocal commitment and effort from followers. Negative behaviors (criticism, neglect, distrust) produce withdrawal and resistance.

## Research Evidence & Effectiveness Data

Transformational leadership is one of the most extensively researched leadership theories, with over 30 years of empirical validation across industries, cultures, and organizational levels.

### Meta-Analysis Results

| Study | Sample | Key Finding | Effect Size |
|-------|--------|-------------|-------------|
| **Judge & Piccolo (2004)** | 87 studies, *Journal of Applied Psychology* | Corrected correlations between transformational leadership and key outcomes: follower job satisfaction (r = .58), motivation (r = .53), leader job performance (r = .27), group/org performance (r = .26), rated leader effectiveness (r = .44) | r = .26 to .58 across criteria |
| **Lowe, Kroeck & Sivasubramaniam (1996)** | 39 studies, *Leadership Quarterly* | Transformational leadership predicts effectiveness beyond transactional leadership; stronger effects in public vs. private sector; subordinate ratings show higher correlations than organizational measures | Transformational r = .71 (subordinate-rated effectiveness) |
| **Wang, Oh, Courtright & Colbert (2011)** | 113 studies, *Journal of Applied Psychology* | Transformational leadership positively related to individual-level performance (task: rho = .21; contextual: rho = .30) and team-level performance (rho = .33); effects hold across criteria types and study designs | rho = .21 to .33 |
| **DeGroot, Kiker & Cross (2000)** | 20 studies | Charisma (idealized influence + inspirational motivation) predicts leader effectiveness (r = .35) and subordinate work attitudes (r = .43) | r = .35 to .43 |

**Key takeaway:** Across thousands of leaders and followers worldwide, transformational leadership consistently explains 7-34% of variance in outcomes that matter (satisfaction, motivation, performance, effectiveness).

### The Augmentation Effect (Bass's Augmentation Hypothesis)

A foundational finding in the Full-Range Leadership research: **transformational leadership builds ON TOP of effective transactional leadership, not instead of it.**

- Bass (1985) proposed and subsequent meta-analyses confirmed that transformational leadership predicts outcomes *above and beyond* transactional leadership (contingent reward).
- Judge & Piccolo (2004) found that contingent reward itself has a strong corrected correlation with leader effectiveness (r = .39) — effective transactional leadership is the *foundation*.
- The practical implication: **you cannot skip transactional fundamentals** (clear goals, fair rewards, performance monitoring) and jump to transformational behaviors. Leaders who attempt transformational leadership without transactional competence are perceived as disorganized visionaries.

**Formula:** `Effective Leadership = Strong Transactional Base + Transformational Amplification`

**The augmentation hierarchy:**
1. First, eliminate passive/avoidant behaviors (laissez-faire, passive MBE)
2. Then, establish solid transactional behaviors (contingent reward, active MBE)
3. Finally, layer on transformational behaviors (4 I's) for performance beyond expectations

### CLT Training Effectiveness

| Study | Design | Key Finding | Magnitude of Effect |
|-------|--------|-------------|---------------------|
| **Antonakis, Fenley & Liechti (2011)** | Field experiment, managers in Swiss firm, *Academy of Management Learning & Education* | Managers trained in CLTs were rated significantly higher on leadership by their followers; charisma is a *learnable skill*, not an innate trait | ~60% improvement in observer-rated charismatic leadership |
| **Antonakis, Bastardoz, Jacquart & Shamir (2016)** | Review paper, *Leadership Quarterly* | Consolidates evidence that charismatic signaling can be systematically taught; proposes "charisma as signal" framework | N/A (theoretical integration) |
| **Barling, Weber & Kelloway (1996)** | Randomized field experiment, branch managers in Canadian bank, *Journal of Applied Psychology* | One of the first experimental demonstrations that transformational leadership training significantly increased followers' organizational commitment and unit-level financial performance | Trained managers' subordinates showed significantly higher commitment; branches showed improved financial performance |
| **Dvir, Eden, Avolio & Shamir (2002)** | Longitudinal field experiment, Israeli military, *Academy of Management Journal* | Transformational leadership training had direct effects on followers' development and indirect effects on followers' performance | Trained leaders' followers scored higher on self-actualization, extra effort, and critical-independent approach |

**Key takeaway:** Charisma and transformational leadership are *trainable competencies*. The "born leader" myth is empirically unsupported — structured training in CLTs and the 4 I's produces measurable improvements in leader ratings and follower outcomes.

### Organizational Impact Data

| Outcome Domain | Research Finding | Source |
|----------------|------------------|--------|
| **Employee Engagement** | Transformational leadership is among the strongest predictors of employee engagement; meta-analytic estimates show r = .45-.58 between transformational leadership and follower satisfaction/engagement | Judge & Piccolo (2004); Harter, Schmidt & Hayes (2002) |
| **Turnover Reduction** | Transformational leadership is negatively associated with turnover intentions (r = -.30 to -.40); the individualized consideration dimension is the strongest predictor of retention | Wells & Peachey (2011); Waldman, Carter & Hom (2015) |
| **Innovation & Creativity** | Intellectual stimulation significantly predicts follower creativity (r = .28-.37); transformational leaders create psychological safety that enables risk-taking and experimentation | Shin & Zhou (2003); Jung, Chow & Wu (2003) |
| **Safety Outcomes** | Transformational leadership predicts safety climate, safety compliance, and reduced workplace injuries; Barling's research established the leadership-safety link | Barling, Loughlin & Kelloway (2002); Clarke (2013): meta-analytic r = .34 for safety climate |
| **Organizational Citizenship Behaviors (OCB)** | Transformational leadership predicts OCBs (extra-role behaviors) above and beyond transactional leadership; followers voluntarily go beyond job requirements | Wang et al. (2011): rho = .30 for contextual performance |
| **Leadership Development ROI** | Industry benchmarks suggest well-designed leadership programs yield 4:1 to 6:1 ROI; however, measurement quality varies widely. Programs with behavioral practice (like CLT training) show stronger transfer than lecture-only programs | Phillips & Phillips (2007); Avolio, Avey & Quisenberry (2010): estimated utility of $2,026-$54,803 per leader for transformational leadership interventions |

### Measurement: Multifactor Leadership Questionnaire (MLQ)

The **MLQ Form 5X** (Bass & Avolio, 1995; current edition: Mind Garden, Inc.) is the most widely used instrument for measuring Full-Range Leadership behaviors. It has been validated in thousands of studies across 100+ countries.

**MLQ Scale Structure (45 items, 5-point frequency scale):**

| Scale Category | Scale Name | Abbreviation | Items | What It Measures |
|----------------|-----------|--------------|-------|------------------|
| **Transformational** | Idealized Influence (Attributed) | II-A | 4 | Follower attributions of leader charisma (trust, admiration) |
| **Transformational** | Idealized Influence (Behavioral) | II-B | 4 | Leader behaviors demonstrating conviction, values, ethical conduct |
| **Transformational** | Inspirational Motivation | IM | 4 | Articulating a compelling vision, expressing optimism, energizing |
| **Transformational** | Intellectual Stimulation | IS | 4 | Challenging assumptions, encouraging creativity, reframing problems |
| **Transformational** | Individualized Consideration | IC | 4 | Coaching, mentoring, attending to individual needs |
| **Transactional** | Contingent Reward | CR | 4 | Setting clear expectations, providing rewards for performance |
| **Transactional** | Management-by-Exception (Active) | MBE-A | 4 | Monitoring for deviations and taking corrective action |
| **Transactional** | Management-by-Exception (Passive) | MBE-P | 4 | Intervening only when problems become serious |
| **Non-Leadership** | Laissez-Faire | LF | 4 | Avoidance of leadership responsibility and decision-making |
| **Outcome** | Extra Effort | EE | 3 | Followers' willingness to try harder |
| **Outcome** | Effectiveness | EFF | 4 | Leader's perceived effectiveness at meeting organizational requirements |
| **Outcome** | Satisfaction | SAT | 2 | Followers' satisfaction with the leader |

**Scoring interpretation:**
- Scores range from 0 (Not at all) to 4 (Frequently, if not always)
- **Ideal Full-Range profile:** High transformational (3.0+), moderate-high contingent reward (2.5+), moderate MBE-A (1.5-2.5), low MBE-P (<1.5), very low LF (<1.0)
- The MLQ produces both *self-ratings* and *rater (follower) ratings*; discrepancies between self and rater scores are diagnostically valuable

### Exercise 1: CLT Practice Drill

**Objective:** Write and deliver a 1-minute persuasive message using at least 5 of the 9 verbal CLTs plus 2+ non-verbal CLTs.

**Setup (5 min):**
1. Choose a topic: a change initiative you want to champion at work, a project you want resources for, or a vision for your team's future
2. Draft a 1-minute speech (~150 words) that incorporates at minimum:
   - 1 metaphor or analogy
   - 1 story or anecdote (can be brief — even 2 sentences)
