# Pitch Deck Creation Reference

## The Four C's Principle

Every element of the pitch must be:
- **Clear**: Immediately understandable, no jargon without explanation
- **Credible**: Backed by evidence, realistic assumptions, honest about risks
- **Compelling**: Creates urgency, shows significant opportunity, emotional hook
- **Concise**: No wasted words, 7-minute constraint demands precision

## Slide-by-Slide Guide

### Slide 1: Title/Hook (10 seconds)
- Company name and one-line description
- Logo/visual identity
- Founder name and contact

One-liner formula: "[Company] helps [target customer] [achieve outcome] by [unique approach]."

### Slide 2: Problem Statement (30 seconds)
Must include:
- Clear articulation of who has the problem
- Quantified pain (cost, time, frustration)
- Why existing solutions fail
- Evidence the problem is real (quotes, data)

Bad: "People need better deodorant."
Good: "31,000 affluent women in our target ZIP codes lack access to sophisticated wine experiences—they're underserved by a $441B market growing at 5% annually."

### Slide 3: Solution/Value Proposition (30 seconds)
- What you offer (be specific)
- How it solves the problem
- Key differentiator in one sentence
- Demo/visual if applicable

Avoid: Feature lists
Use: Outcome-focused benefits

### Slide 4: Market Opportunity (30 seconds)
Required elements:
- TAM with source
- SAM with logic for how you defined it
- SOM with realistic capture rate
- Bottom-up calculation shown

Example:
"Global deodorant: $25B TAM
Youth natural segment: $2B SAM
Target capture (Year 3): $1.2M SOM (0.06%)
Based on: 417K target households × 1% penetration × $3 unit price × 12 months"

### Slide 5: Business Model (30 seconds)
Cover:
- Revenue streams (prioritize by size)
- Pricing structure
- Unit economics snapshot
- Key margins

Visual: Simple diagram of money flow or table of economics

### Slide 6: Go-to-Market (30 seconds)
Address:
- How you acquire customers
- Channel strategy
- Launch sequence (phases)
- Customer acquisition cost target

Show you've thought about the hard part: getting customers.

### Slide 7: Competitive Landscape (30 seconds)
Required:
- Named competitors (never say "no competition")
- Positioning matrix or comparison table
- Your sustainable advantage (moat)
- Why you win

Framework: 2x2 matrix with meaningful axes

### Slide 8: Traction/Validation (45 seconds)
Most important slide for early-stage. Include whatever you have:
- Revenue/users/customers
- Growth rate (month-over-month)
- Customer testimonials
- Pilot results
- Pre-orders
- Letters of intent
- Advisory relationships

If pre-revenue: Focus on validation activities, customer discovery insights, MVP test results

### Slide 9: Team (30 seconds)
For each key person:
- Name and role
- One-line relevant background
- Why this team wins

Address gaps honestly: "Seeking CTO with [specific expertise]"

Show advisory board if strong.

### Slide 10: Financial Projections (30 seconds)
3-year summary:
- Revenue
- Key expenses
- Path to profitability
- Break-even timing

Include: Key assumptions stated
Avoid: Hockey stick without justification

### Slide 11: The Ask (30 seconds)
Be specific:
- Amount raising
- Use of funds (pie chart or bullets)
- Key milestones funds will achieve
- Timeline to next raise/profitability

Example: "$500K for 18-month runway: 40% product development, 30% marketing, 20% operations, 10% reserve. Targets: 1,000 customers, break-even by month 14."

## Speaker Notes Guidelines

Time per slide: 20-30 seconds maximum (7 minutes ÷ 11 slides ≈ 38 seconds average, minus transitions)

Structure for each:
1. Opening hook (5 seconds)
2. Key point with data (15 seconds)
3. Transition to next (5 seconds)

Write in complete sentences but practice delivery until natural.

## Q&A Preparation

Anticipate questions on:
- How did you arrive at your market size?
- What's your customer acquisition strategy specifically?
- Who are your competitors and why do you win?
- Why this team?
- What are the biggest risks?
- How will you use the funds?
- What's your exit strategy?

Prepare: 2-3 sentence crisp answers for each.

## Common Pitch Mistakes

1. **Too much time on problem**: Investors know problems exist; get to solution faster
2. **No traction slide**: Even early-stage needs validation evidence
3. **Vague market sizing**: "Multi-billion dollar market" without specifics
4. **No competition slide**: Every business has competition
5. **Feature-focused**: Talk outcomes, not features
6. **Too many words per slide**: 20 words maximum per slide
7. **Reading slides**: Know your content; slides are visual support
8. **No clear ask**: Always end with specific request

## Presentation Delivery

- Practice minimum 10 times with timer
- Record yourself and review
- Present to non-experts for clarity feedback
- Prepare for technical issues (offline backup)
- Dress appropriately for audience
- Make eye contact with everyone in room
- Slow down when nervous (most common issue)
