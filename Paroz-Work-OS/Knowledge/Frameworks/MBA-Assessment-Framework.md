# New Venture Assessment Framework Reference

## Sahlman's Framework Deep Dive

### The People

Questions to answer:
- Where are the founders from?
- Where have they been educated?
- Where have they worked—and for whom?
- What have they accomplished—professionally and personally—in the past?
- What is their reputation within the business community?
- What experience do they have that is directly relevant to the opportunity they are pursuing?
- What skills, abilities, and knowledge do they have?
- How realistic are they about the venture's chances for success and the tribulations it will face?
- Who else needs to be on the team?
- Are they prepared to recruit high-quality people?
- How will they respond to adversity?
- Do they have the mettle to make the inevitable hard choices?
- How committed are they to this venture?
- What are their motivations?

Team assessment proxies when information is limited:
- Left stable job (commitment signal)
- Invested personal capital
- Mortgaged house
- Recruited quality co-founders
- Built relevant advisory board
- Prior startup experience (even failed)

### The Opportunity

Key questions:
- Who is the customer?
- How does the customer make decisions about buying this product?
- To what degree is the product a compelling purchase for the customer?
- How will the product be priced?
- How will the venture reach all identified customer segments?
- How much does it cost to acquire and retain a customer?
- How long before the customer generates profit?
- How well does the venture protect the profit stream from competitors?
- What are the margins on the product/service?
- How capital intensive is the business?

Economics indicators:
- Gross margins >40% (healthy)
- Multiple revenue streams
- Recurring revenue components
- Low customer concentration
- Clear path to profitability

### The Context

Macro factors to assess:
- Interest rates and capital availability
- Regulatory trends (favorable/unfavorable changes)
- Demographic shifts
- Technology enablers
- Economic cycle position
- Social/cultural trends

Industry-specific:
- Growth rate of the overall market
- Competitive intensity
- Supplier and buyer power
- Threat of substitutes
- Barriers to entry

Timing considerations:
- Is the window of opportunity opening, fully open, or closing?
- What needs to change for opportunity to improve?
- What external events could derail the opportunity?

### Risk and Reward

Risk categories:
1. **Technology risk**: Can it be built?
2. **Market risk**: Will customers buy?
3. **Execution risk**: Can the team deliver?
4. **Financial risk**: Can it be funded to success?
5. **Competitive risk**: Will others capture the opportunity?

For each major risk:
- Probability of occurrence
- Impact if it occurs
- Mitigation strategies
- Team's ability to respond

## First Level Screen Application Guide

### Problem/Customer Deep Dive

Evidence of real problem:
- Customer interviews (100+ for validation)
- Surveys with quantified pain points
- Search volume for related solutions
- Industry reports citing the problem
- Failed previous attempts (shows demand)

Market attractiveness criteria:
- Size: >$1B TAM for venture-scale
- Growth: >10% CAGR preferred
- Fragmentation: No single player >30% share
- Underserved segments identifiable

### Timing Assessment

Favorable timing signals:
- Recent regulatory change creating opportunity
- Technology cost crossing threshold
- Demographic cohort reaching buying age
- Platform shift (mobile, AI, etc.) enabling new models
- Incumbent disruption creating market gaps

Unfavorable timing signals:
- Market dominated by well-funded incumbents
- Technology not yet mature
- Customer behavior change required (slow)
- Regulatory uncertainty

### Money Making Analysis

Unit economics requirements:
- LTV:CAC ratio >3:1
- CAC payback <18 months
- Gross margin >50% for software, >30% for physical goods
- Contribution margin positive

Cash flow considerations:
- Inventory requirements
- Payment terms (customer vs supplier)
- Capital intensity
- Seasonal patterns

### Team Fit Evaluation

Ideal team characteristics:
- Domain expertise (know the industry)
- Functional coverage (tech, sales, ops)
- Network access (customers, partners, talent)
- Startup experience (preferably successful)
- Complementary skills (not all same background)

Common team gaps (and solutions):
- No technical founder → CTO hire or technical advisor
- No sales experience → Fractional sales leader
- No industry connections → Strategic advisor with network
- No startup experience → Experienced operators as advisors

## Hypothesis-Driven Approach

### Formulating Testable Hypotheses

Good hypothesis format:
"We believe [target customer] will [action] because [reason], and we will know we are right when [measurable outcome]."

Categories of hypotheses:
1. Value hypothesis: Does the product solve a real problem?
2. Growth hypothesis: Can we acquire customers economically?
3. Execution hypothesis: Can we deliver at scale?

### MVP Test Types

| Test Type | Best For | Cost/Speed |
|-----------|----------|------------|
| Landing page | Demand validation | Low/Fast |
| Concierge | Service model | Medium/Medium |
| Wizard of Oz | Tech product | Medium/Medium |
| Prototype | Physical product | Higher/Slower |
| Pilot | B2B solutions | Higher/Slower |

### Decision Rules

**Persevere**: Hypothesis validated, metrics trending positive, team confident
**Pivot**: Core hypothesis rejected but related opportunity identified, team willing to change
**Perish**: Fundamental assumptions invalid, no viable pivot, resources depleted

Common pivot types:
- Customer segment pivot
- Customer need pivot
- Channel pivot
- Technology pivot
- Revenue model pivot
- Platform pivot
