# Paroz Master Communication Protocol
*Executive Communication System Integrating Cornell EMBA Frameworks & Professional Practice*

---

## Executive Summary

This protocol synthesizes Paroz's proven communication patterns from 20+ professional emails with Cornell EMBA's advanced frameworks (SUCCESS, PREP, SBI, Monroe's Motivated Sequence) and executive presence principles. The result: a comprehensive communication system for emails, presentations, stakeholder management, and leadership presence.

**Core Philosophy:** "Brevity for efficiency, structure for clarity, warmth through consistency, stories for impact."

## Part I: Communication Foundation

### 1.1 Three-Tier Communication System

#### Tier 1 - Routine Communications (70%)
- **Length:** 30-75 words
- **Structure:** Direct answer → Action → Gratitude
- **Response Time:** <24 hours
- **Framework:** BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front)

#### Tier 2 - Professional External (25%)  
- **Length:** 50-100 words
- **Structure:** Purpose → Attachment → Offer help → Thanks
- **Tone Evolution:** Formal → Semi-formal → Warm (3-4 months)
- **Framework:** PREP for key points

#### Tier 3 - Leadership/Strategic (5%)
- **Length:** 400-700 words
- **Structure:** Mission → Philosophy → Focus Areas → Operations → Managing Me
- **Pre-send:** Seek trusted advisor review
- **Framework:** Monroe's Motivated Sequence for persuasion

### 1.2 Core Communication Values

1. **Directness:** "Answer the questions – short, to the point"
2. **No Surprises:** "Bad news gets worse with age"
3. **Accountability:** "What, who, and when" for every action
4. **Team Language:** Always "we" not "they"
5. **Executive Presence:** Gravitas + Communication + Appearance

## Part II: Master Frameworks

### 2.1 SUCCESS Principles (Made to Stick)

Every communication must pass the SUCCESS checklist:

- **S**imple: If everything's important, nothing's important
- **U**nexpected: Create demand before supply (parachute in, don't preamble)
- **C**oncrete: Paint mental pictures, bring reality into the room
- **C**redible: Cite sources, provide evidence, show don't tell
- **E**motional: Connect with what audience cares about (WIIFM)
- **S**tories: 63% recall vs 5% for data alone

**Implementation:** Stories are the entrée, not garnish. Use 3:1 story-to-exposition ratio.

### 2.2 PREP Framework (High-Pressure Response)

For Q&A, meetings, impromptu situations:

```
P - Point: State conclusion upfront
R - Reason: Provide rationale 
E - Example: Specific data/story
P - Point: Restate for emphasis
```

**Example:**
"We should migrate to Databricks [Point]. Our current SSIS processes are failing weekly [Reason]. Last Tuesday's outage cost us $50K [Example]. Databricks provides the stability we need [Point]."

### 2.3 SBI Framework (Feedback & Difficult Conversations)

```
S - Situation: Specific when/where
B - Behavior: Observable facts only
I - Impact: Effect on team/business
```

**Example:**
"In yesterday's sprint review [Situation], three action items had no owners assigned [Behavior]. This delays our release by a week [Impact]."

### 2.4 Monroe's Motivated Sequence (Persuasive Presentations)

Structure for any persuasion:

1. **Introduction - RAMP**
   - Rapport Builder
   - Attention Getter  
   - Message/Purpose
   - Plan

2. **Problem Description**
   - State problem
   - Illustrate with examples
   - Show direct audience impact

3. **Present Solution**
   - State solution clearly
   - Explain benefits
   - Address objections preemptively

4. **Visualize Results**
   - Positive method: "What wonderful outcomes..."
   - Negative method: "Without this, we face..."
   - Contrast method: Compare both

5. **Close - Request Action**
   - Precise, specific ask
   - WIIFM reminder
   - **Bold the next action**

### 2.5 STAR Moments (Presentation Impact)

Create one memorable moment per presentation:

- **S**hocking statistics
- **T**angible demonstrations  
- **A**nalogies that resonate
- **R**emarkable visuals

**Example:** Slap down 519-page audit stack: "This is the road to hell paved with good intentions."

## Part III: Executive Presence Formula

### 3.1 The New Executive Presence (2024 Standards)

#### Gravitas (76% of EP)
- **Confidence:** Grace under fire
- **Decisiveness:** Clear ownership of decisions
- **Inclusiveness:** Listen-to-learn orientation
- **Vision:** Paint the future state
- **Integrity:** Your word is your bond

#### Communication (66% of EP)
- **Superior Speaking:** BLUF + PREP + Stories
- **Command of Room/Zoom:** Physical and vocal presence
- **Active Listening:** Level 3 empathic listening
- **Read the Audience:** Adapt in real-time
- **Authenticity:** Vulnerability with boundaries

#### Appearance (37% of EP)
- **Polished Look:** Appropriate to context
- **Fitness/Vigor:** Energy and engagement
- **Virtual Presence:** Camera-ready, good lighting
- **Body Language:** Open, purposeful gestures

### 3.2 Vocal Presence Protocols

**Avoid Weak Language:**
- Hedging: "I think," "maybe," "perhaps"
- Fillers: "um," "like," "you know"
- Up-speak: Rising intonation on statements

**Use Power Language:**
- Active voice: "I will deliver" not "It will be delivered"
- Specific commitments: "By Friday 3pm" not "soon"
- Decisive verbs: "execute," "drive," "deliver"

### 3.3 Q&A Mastery Framework

**When You Don't Know:**
"I don't have that data, but here's what I know... I'll follow up by [specific time]."

**When Challenged:**
"I appreciate your perspective. My findings show [evidence]. Let's discuss offline."

**When Dominated:**
"Great point. Let's hear from others. [Name], your thoughts?"

**Plant Questions if Silent:**
"A common question is... Let me address that."

## Part IV: Email & Written Communication

### 4.1 Subject Line Formulas

**Decision Required:**
`[DECISION BY DATE] Topic - Impact if Delayed`

**Action Required:**
`[ACTION REQUIRED] Task - Owner - Deadline`

**Update/FYI:**
`[Project Name] Status: Green/Yellow/Red - Key Point`

**Escalation:**
`[ESCALATION] Blocker - Need Executive Decision by [Date]`

### 4.2 Email Templates

#### Quick Professional Response (25-50 words)
```
[Greeting matched to relationship],

[Direct answer/acknowledgment]
[Action item with deadline]

Thanks,
[Name]
```

#### Executive Decision Request (200-400 words)
```
[Executive Name],

**Decision Required:** [One sentence]
**Recommendation:** [Your recommendation]
**Deadline:** [Date/time]

**Context:** [2-3 sentences background]

**Options Analysis:**
| Option | Pros | Cons | Cost |
|--------|------|------|------|
| A | | | |
| B (Recommended) | | | |
| C | | | |

**Impact of Delay:** [Consequence]

Available to discuss [provide 2-3 time slots].

[Name]
```

#### Stakeholder Update (Weekly)
```
[Stakeholder],

**Project:** [Name] | **Status:** 🟢 On Track

**This Week:**
✅ [Achievement 1]
✅ [Achievement 2]

**Next Week:**
🎯 [Priority 1]
🎯 [Priority 2]

**Blockers:** None [or specific issue + mitigation]

**Need from You:** [Specific ask or "No action needed"]

[Name]
```

### 4.3 Relationship Development Arc

**Month 0-1:** "Dear [Full Name]," → "Sincerely,"
**Month 2-3:** "Good morning [First Name]," → "Best regards,"  
**Month 4+:** "Hi [First Name]," → Personal touches, occasional emoji

Never rush warmth. Build through consistency.

## Part V: Presentation Excellence

### 5.1 Presentation Structure

#### Opening (10% of time)
- **Start with conclusion** (not agenda)
- **Hook:** Question, story, shocking statistic
- **Promise:** What they'll gain
- **Roadmap:** Max 3 points

#### Middle (80% of time)
- **Stories first, data second**
- **One idea per slide**
- **Show, don't tell** (demos, examples)
- **Rule of 3:** Three main points maximum

#### Close (10% of time)
- **Reframe opening** (callback)
- **Clear call-to-action**
- **WIIFM reminder**
- **Bold next step**

### 5.2 Slide Design Principles

**Text Rules:**
- Maximum 6 words per line
- Maximum 6 lines per slide
- NO reading from slides
- Images > bullets

**Visual Hierarchy:**
- Title: Key message (not description)
- Body: Supporting visual/data
- Takeaway: One-line conclusion

### 5.3 Persuasive Presentation Appeals

Balance three appeals throughout:

1. **Ethos (Credibility):** Your expertise, citations, track record
2. **Logos (Logic):** Data, evidence, ROI calculations
3. **Pathos (Emotion):** Stories, vision, personal impact

**Golden Ratio:** 40% Pathos, 40% Logos, 20% Ethos

### 5.4 Virtual Presentation Adjustments

- **Engagement every 3 minutes** (poll, question, demo)
- **Camera at eye level**, good lighting
- **Standing desk** for energy
- **Slides: 30% less content** than in-person
- **Record for async viewing**

## Part VI: Meeting Leadership

### 6.1 Meeting Communication Cadence

**T-24 hours:** Send agenda with:
- Objective (decision vs discussion)
- Pre-read materials
- Required attendees
- Time allocations

**T-0:** Run meeting:
- Start/end on time
- Focus on decisions
- Capture "what, who, when"
- No sidebars

**T+4 hours:** Send summary:
- Decisions made
- Action items table
- Next meeting date

### 6.2 Meeting Agenda Template

```
**Meeting:** [Name]
**Objective:** [What we must accomplish]

**Agenda:**
1. [Topic] - [5 min] - [Owner] - Decision
2. [Topic] - [10 min] - [Owner] - Discussion  
3. [Topic] - [5 min] - [Owner] - Decision

**Decisions Needed:**
- [Specific decision with options]

**Pre-Read:** [Link to materials]
```

- **Length:** 100-250 words
- **Format:** BLUF + Options + Recommendation
- **Visual:** One chart/table maximum

- **Style:** Peer-level, technical depth

- **Frequency:** Daily standups + weekly deep-dives

#### Cross-Functional Partners
- **Style:** Collaborative, solutions-focused
- **Format:** Status + blockers + needs
- **Frequency:** Weekly sync + ad-hoc
